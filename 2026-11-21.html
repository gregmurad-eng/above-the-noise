Lead Story

Signal says the OS AI wave is an existential threat — and starts blocking it — Signal (Meredith Whittaker)[1]

Twenty-four hours after Google shipped Gemini computer use on the Mac,[2] Signal President Meredith Whittaker posted a blog entry that reads less like a product update than like a declaration of posture against the three companies that build the operating systems Signal runs on.[1] On iOS 27 Signal now ships a Block Siri AI setting and blocks screenshots inside the app and in the App Switcher by default. On Android, Signal auto-enables its existing Screen Security feature when it detects an assistant trying to read the screen.[1] Users can turn either off; if they do, Signal shows a notification naming what they just exposed.

Whittaker's framing is the move. Gemini on Android, Siri's Onscreen Awareness on iOS 27, and Microsoft's equivalents on Windows now “live inside the operating system” and “read whatever is on your screen” — which, she writes, is “an existential threat to Signal, and our ability to responsibly uphold our mission.”[1] End-to-end encryption protects a message in transit; it cannot protect its contents after a system assistant has captured them from pixels on a screen it is entitled to read.

Google announced Gemini computer use on the Mac on 2026-11-19 via blog.google: the assistant clicks and types inside native Mac apps and browser windows in an isolated background window with a live preview, pausing for logins, payments and sensitive actions.[2] Reuters-parent reporting and TechCrunch covered the launch the same day.[3] That is the context Signal is reacting to: a mainstream, paid, OS-integrated agent that routinely crosses the trust boundary between an app's data and the operating system. The two primary sources — Signal's and Google's — agree on the capability and disagree on whether it is a feature or a threat.

Whittaker also calls her own fix “incomplete.” Blocking screenshots does not block an assistant from reaching Signal content through the phone's file system or other OS-level routes.[1] The real ask in the post is to Apple, Google and Microsoft: publish documented, developer-facing opt-outs for apps that do not want their data read by system AI, and publish documentation describing how those agents actually work. Neither exists today. Which is the point: the first major privacy brand has put on the record that the platforms owe it answers before the next OS release, not after.

So what: if your app handles anything users assume is private — messages, health, legal, HR, financial — audit your mobile and desktop builds this week for the two surfaces Signal just closed. Add FLAG_SECURE on sensitive Android screens, set isScreenCaptureAllowed = false on iOS 27 sensitive views, hide content in the app switcher, and document the exact OS mechanism you rely on — because when a user asks you whether Gemini or Siri AI can read your screen, “probably not” stops being an acceptable answer the week Signal makes it a public question.

CISA adds LiteLLM guardrail sandbox escape (CVE-2026-40217) to the KEV catalog, Dec 2 federal deadline — CISA[4]

On 2026-11-18 CISA added CVE-2026-40217 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog — a sandbox escape in the /guardrails/test_custom_code endpoint of the LiteLLM AI gateway that lets any authenticated user run code inside a proxy process that, in the default Docker image, runs as root.[4][5] Affected: LiteLLM 1.81.8 through 1.83.10; fixed in 1.83.11 (released 2026-11-15). The federal remediation deadline is 2026-12-02. This is the fourth LiteLLM CVE in KEV in 2026.[5]

So what: if you run LiteLLM in front of any model, bump to 1.83.11+ before 2026-12-02 and audit any virtual key with guardrail-test privileges — the exploit is from a virtual key, not an unauthenticated internet attacker, which means your blast radius is whoever holds a key on your proxy.
