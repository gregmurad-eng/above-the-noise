The Transformer's Architect Walks Out of Google — and Into OpenAI

The talent war just got its biggest casualty since Karpathy

Noam Shazeer — co-author of the 2017 paper "Attention Is All You Need," the eight-page document that gave the world the Transformer and, by extension, every large language model running today — has left Google DeepMind to join OpenAI as Lead for Architecture Research. The announcement landed June 18, and in an industry that has normalized billion-dollar poaching, it still managed to feel like a tremor.

The role matters as much as the name. Shazeer won't be managing a product or shipping a chatbot feature; he'll own the physical neural-network structures underneath all of OpenAI's models — the next-generation architectures meant to succeed the very design he helped invent. Sam Altman framed the hire on X as "only 10 years in the making," calling Shazeer one of the people he has most wanted to work with since OpenAI's founding. That's not recruiting theater. It's a statement that OpenAI believes the next leap won't come from more data or more GPUs alone, but from rethinking the architecture itself.

The sting for Google is sharper because of the price tag. In August 2024, Google brought Shazeer back through a roughly $2.7 billion arrangement tied to Character.AI, the startup he co-founded after leaving the first time. Less than two years and billions of dollars later, he's gone again — and he's not the only one. His exit came within days of a Nobel laureate departing for Anthropic, a one-two punch that has Google bleeding foundational talent at exactly the moment it's trying to convince the market that Gemini 3.5 is the frontier.

For solo builders, the meta-signal is what counts. When the people who invented the current paradigm are being paid fortunes to go invent the next one, it tells you the incumbents themselves think the Transformer era has a ceiling. The tools you build on this year are not the tools you'll build on in two. Architecture is back on the table as a competitive variable, not a settled question.

Analysis: Talent moves are the truest leading indicator in AI — more honest than benchmarks, which everyone games, and more honest than launch dates, which everyone slips. Shazeer didn't chase a title; he chased a mandate to redesign the foundation. Watch where the architecture researchers cluster over the next 18 months. That's where the next platform shift is being quietly assembled, long before it shows up in a model card.

The Briefs

White House issues AI innovation-and-security executive order

The June 2 executive order, "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," sets up a voluntary federal framework for "frontier" models and an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse to remediate software vulnerabilities at scale. Crucially, it expressly bars any mandatory licensing or pre-clearance to release a model — innovation-friendly on paper, with agency deliverables due July 2 and August 1.

Microsoft ships its own models to cut the OpenAI cord

At Build, Microsoft unveiled MAI-Code-1-Flash, a model that turns plain-language descriptions into app and website source code, alongside MAI-Thinking-1, a reasoning model. The throughline is independence: lower-cost, in-house options that reduce reliance on OpenAI for developers building on Azure.

Zhipu's GLM-5.2 lands under MIT license with a 1M-token window

Released June 13, GLM-5.2 pairs a Mixture-of-Experts architecture with a usable one-million-token context window — and ships under a permissive MIT license. For builders, that's a rare combination: frontier-scale context plus the freedom to deploy commercially without strings.
