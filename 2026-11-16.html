Lead Story

Anthropic prices its IPO at a $1.4 trillion ceiling — CNBC, Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance[1][2][3]

Amended S-1/A filed with the SEC on 10–11 November 2026; roadshow live; trading expected to open on Nasdaq Thursday 19 November under ticker ANT.[1][4]

Anthropic’s amended S-1/A sets a $70–$78 price range on 825 million shares, a top-of-range fully-diluted valuation of about $1.4 trillion, and a trading debut on Nasdaq this Thursday.[1] Nine months of revenue through September totalled roughly $33.2 billion — a 12x year-over-year jump — and the filing reports an October run-rate of approximately $81 billion.[1] The IPO is paired with a $26 billion private placement from seven cornerstone buyers: Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft, and TSMC.[1][5] Last week TSMC’s board separately filed an SEC Form 6-K authorising up to $500 million each into Anthropic and OpenAI — a publicly reported chip foundry taking equity in both of its largest American model customers at the same moment.[5]

The number that matters to builders is not the headline valuation but the composition of that cornerstone list. In the IPO roadshow video dissected by Simon Willison, co-founder Tom Brown describes Anthropic training and serving Claude across four distinct silicon stacks — AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, Nvidia GPUs, and AMD GPUs — and three of those four vendors show up as cornerstone investors, alongside the foundry (TSMC) that fabricates for all of them.[6] This is the first time a frontier-lab IPO has been publicly staged as a supply-chain coalition, not a bet on a single hyperscaler.

The counter-narrative is uglier. OpenAI’s own quarterly data, reported to investors the same week Anthropic filed, shows Q3 revenue of $10 billion — a first — against a $10.9 billion operating loss.[7] Anthropic’s S-1/A does not disclose comparable operating losses in its marketed summary. Both labs are shipping growth at a rate that genuinely compounds, and both are losing roughly a dollar for every dollar of revenue they take in, on compute they cannot presently make themselves. The IPO is therefore a public test of whether Wall Street will fund the next round of that trade. Earlier reports had the deal priced near $2 trillion; the final range is roughly half that, which suggests even the bankers blinked.[4]

Also notable: the retail allocation is reportedly near 20%, roughly triple the typical IPO allocation.[1] If ANT opens weak on Thursday, it will be weak in front of retail investors, not just institutions — a political variable the IPO class of 2026 has not yet had to price.

If you ship on Claude, the Anthropic compute story (four silicon vendors across two hyperscalers plus Nvidia plus AMD) is now a public document — use it when asking Anthropic about capacity and regional availability for Q1, because “we’re just on GCP” is no longer a credible answer from either side of the call.

The Briefs

NIST publishes a formal attack taxonomy for agentic AI — NIST (primary)[8]

Initial public draft NIST SP 2400-102e2026, released 9 November 2026; comments due 18 December 2026.

NIST’s draft is the first US federal document to name — as standards terminology — memory poisoning, tool-chain abuse, agent-to-agent prompt injection, and skill supply-chain attacks as distinct classes alongside the already-codified generative-AI attack types.[8] Public comment is open through 18 December; what ships after that becomes the vocabulary your government auditor will use next year.

If you run an agent in production, map your tool permissions, retrieval sources, and inter-agent messages onto the NIST categories this month — then file a comment. The labs who file comments tend to end up with the terminology they can defend against.

TSMC board authorises up to $500M each into Anthropic and OpenAI — SEC Form 6-K (primary)[5]

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company 6-K filing, 10 November 2026.

TSMC’s 6-K authorises up to $500M to acquire Anthropic common shares at IPO price, up to $500M in OpenAI preferred stock, and a separate $500M strategic-investments vehicle for the broader semiconductor ecosystem.[5] The foundry is taking equity in both of its largest American model customers at the same moment — a structural decision, not a treasury one.

If you are negotiating capacity on either model in 2027, be aware that your foundry now owns both sides of your supply-chain trade. Ask for pricing clauses that survive a public-company earnings cycle, not a private one.

Google Cloud ships Antigravity for HPC and AlphaEvolve on Cloud Batch at SC26 — HPCwire[9]

Announced 9 November 2026; live demos at SC26 McCormick Place West through 20 November.

Google Cloud paired Antigravity (its agentic development platform) with reusable DeepMind Science Skills — validated-template skills for Slurm, GROMACS, OpenFOAM, Devito, and AlphaFold 3 — and shipped AlphaEvolve on Cloud Batch, which fans out hundreds of candidate evaluations across single-VM or multi-node MPI task groups.[9] This is the first agentic-coding stack explicitly aimed at national-lab-grade workloads, not SaaS CRUD apps.

EU harmonised standard EN 18286:2026 lands; sandbox implementing rules take effect 1 December — European Commission (primary)[10]

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2487 adopted 10 November 2026; EN 18286:2026 published in the Official Journal.

EN 18286:2026 is the first harmonised standard formally published under the EU AI Act — a quality management system for high-risk AI providers — and about 11 more AI Act standards are in flight.[10] The AI-Act sandbox rules, adopted the same day, bind on 1 December 2026. Both matter because conformance with a harmonised standard creates a presumption of conformance with the regulation itself: it is the compliance path that is cheapest to document.

If you sell any high-risk system into the EU, decide this quarter whether you’ll conform to EN 18286 or negotiate a bespoke conformity route — the former is cheaper and the latter is faster, and you cannot do both.
