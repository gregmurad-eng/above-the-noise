Lead Story

Anthropic prices its IPO on the same page as its chip bills

— Anthropic S-1/A (via TradingView, Forbes, Reuters)

Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow on Tuesday at a band of seventy to seventy-eight dollars a share for 825 million shares, with trading penciled for 19 November on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT[1]. At the top of the band the public offer raises about 64.4 billion dollars at a fully diluted valuation near 1.4 trillion dollars. That is the headline. The organising fact of the filing sits one page over: the same prospectus names seven firms — Google, Nvidia, AMD and Microsoft among them — as buyers of 26 billion dollars in Class N shares in a concurrent private placement[1].

Read that against today's other number. OpenAI told investors its third quarter revenue hit about 10 billion dollars, up roughly fifty percent, against an operating loss of 10.9 billion — a margin of minus one hundred and nine percent on a top line that is still growing fast[2]. The company losing a dollar nine for every dollar of revenue is not the one about to list; the one listing is doing it with its biggest suppliers, three of them chip vendors, named as equity backers in the same document, at the same price, as the public.

The founder-letter frame, delivered on a forty-two-minute roadshow video, is Dario Amodei's claim that cancer, Alzheimer's, heart disease and mental illness "will be relics of the past in, I think, less than a decade"[3]. The S-1 prospectus itself is more cautious, listing those diseases as potential targets without the ten-year clock; Amodei's line is his own, and clinicians are already on record saying the timeline is implausible against trial cycles[3]. It is a mission sentence attached to a capital-markets filing in a way we have not seen before.

The structural read is the quieter one. A year ago Nvidia sold GPUs to Anthropic and reported revenue. Google sold TPUs to Anthropic and reported revenue. Each of those contracts is still on the invoice. What is new, visible in the filing this week, is that the chip suppliers are also named buyers of pre-public stock in the same offering; the capital moving to Anthropic for compute is now partly the same capital moving back as equity. It is a loop. The disclosure value is what matters: a public filing forces each counterparty's own filings to catch up, and the reciprocal commitments — how much compute, for how long, at what price — become material in a way private vendor contracts never are.

If your platform decision across 2027 rests on "which frontier lab is most compute-independent," the answer is now readable from the IPO prospectus and the 10-Qs that will reconcile against it, not from press releases — the capital loop is the supply chain, and both sides will now disclose.
