Lead Story

Anthropic has locked in $517B of compute in eleven months — nearly triple its own December investor budget — as it prepares to file the biggest AI IPO on record — The Information[1]

The Information's Kate Clark reported on Saturday, 6 September, that Anthropic has signed compute-capacity contracts totalling as much as $517 billion since October 2025, locking in at least 14.8 gigawatts of new capacity on top of the roughly 1–2 GW it already runs on[1]. In December 2025, Anthropic showed investors a server-leasing budget of roughly $180B through 2029; nine months later the contracted scale is already about 2.9× that plan[1].

The individual deals — most of them broken by tier-1 press in the weeks before The Information's aggregation — read like a compute-supply cartel forming in fast-motion. Bloomberg reported the $35B Lambda deal anchored at Hut 8's Beacon Point campus in Nueces County, Texas on 31 August[2]. CNBC and TechCrunch, working the same story independently, reported the $45B Nscale deal on 26 August[3][4] — 460 MW leased from Nscale's West Virginia site over six years on Nvidia Vera Rubin silicon. Add ~$100B with AWS over ten years, ~$50B with Fluidstack for own-facility buildout, ~$45B with SpaceX, and the earlier ~$30B/1 GW Microsoft Azure commitment from November, and you get to The Information's aggregate[1]. Bloomberg separately reported on 3 September that Anthropic is finalizing a $15B pre-IPO credit facility to keep the cash side of the ledger moving[5].

The numbers still trail OpenAI's ~$750B plan through 2030, but the shape is what matters: Anthropic's July annualised revenue run-rate was $65B[6]. Even at aggressive growth, the contracted commitments run years ahead of any plausible cash flow — which is why the $15B pre-IPO facility, the confidential S-1 filed in June, and the reported October Nasdaq target are all one story, not four. It also arrives days after CEO Dario Amodei's earlier warning that rivals "don't really understand the risks they're taking"[7] — a line that reads differently now.

The counterpoint from the other side of the trade: on 1 September, Sam Altman told Alex Heath's Sources podcast that he sees "the first signs of what feels to me like unsustainable silliness" — new neoclouds promising "gigantic amounts of compute next year" with no revenue and no buyer[8]. Altman carved his own company out of the warning and put it on everyone else. Anthropic is now on both sides of that sentence: it is the buyer making Fluidstack and Lambda and Nscale into $18B, $30B, $20B companies overnight, and it is the counterparty whose plausible revenue has to hold up the whole tower.

So what: if you're building on Claude, model your 2027–28 pricing off a supplier that has committed ~$517B of cost against a $65B ARR — margin compression is more likely than expansion, and rate-limit generosity is unlikely to widen. Anthropic-primary architectures should have a costed Google-Gemini or open-weight fallback before the S-1 prices, not after.

The Briefs

Anthropic rolls out Enterprise Frontier Safeguards — zero data retention with misuse detection that lives in the customer's own cloud — Anthropic[9]

Announced 1 September alongside Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1, EFS lets enterprise activity data used for misuse monitoring sit in the customer's Amazon S3, Azure Blob or Google Cloud Storage under customer-controlled keys — Anthropic never retains it[9]. Automated systems scan a rolling window for offensive-cyber and bio uplift, and for leaked/stolen credentials. The Register notes the trust burden that comes with it: customers must verify the retention setting actually worked, because Anthropic can't prove a negative on their behalf[10]. Phased rollout this fall; interim ZDR on Fable 5 / 5.1 bridges the gap.

So what: this is the sentence that unblocks Claude in regulated industries — banks, health systems, defence primes — that previously wouldn't ship prompts with PII to a lab-controlled logging path. If your compliance review died on "Anthropic retains logs for 30 days," reopen it.
