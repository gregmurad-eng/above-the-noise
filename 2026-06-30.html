Lead Story

OpenAI previews GPT-5.6 — and splits it into three

OpenAI on June 26 pulled back the curtain on the GPT-5.6 series, what the company calls its most significant architecture change since GPT-5. Rather than ship a single monolith, OpenAI is splitting the family into three named tiers, each tuned to a different point on the cost-capability curve.

At the top sits Sol, the flagship aimed at genuinely hard problems — long-horizon reasoning, research-grade coding, the work where you'd happily pay for the best answer. In the middle is Terra, pitched as the balanced everyday model: OpenAI claims it matches GPT-5.5's quality while costing roughly half as much per token. At the bottom is Luna, the fast, cheap volume model for the high-throughput tasks where latency and price matter more than raw intelligence.

The naming is the tell. For two years the frontier labs competed on a single number that kept climbing; the interesting move now is segmentation. OpenAI is conceding that most production traffic does not need the flagship, and that the real margin war is over the Terra-shaped middle — good-enough quality at a price that makes per-request economics work at scale. Access to the preview is gated, with the company citing the usual safety-evaluation runway before broad availability.

For solo builders, this is the more consequential trend than any benchmark bump. A 2x price cut on a GPT-5.5-class model changes which features are economically viable to ship — background agents, per-user personalization, always-on summarization. If Terra holds up, the calculus for routing cheap calls to Luna and reserving Sol for the hard 5% becomes the default architecture, not a clever optimization. Watch the actual per-token pricing when it lands; the preview framing is generous and the real numbers are what move roadmaps.

The Briefs

US government clears a limited release of Anthropic's Mythos

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick determined "appropriate safeguards are in place" to let trusted partners access Claude Mythos 5, Anthropic's strongest cybersecurity model, after an export block earlier in June over fears it could supercharge attackers. A weaker, guardrailed sibling (Fable 5) ships more broadly with cyber and bio queries blocked.

Google throttles Meta's Gemini compute, rents from SpaceX

Google reportedly capped Meta's access to its models after Meta requested more capacity than Google could supply, delaying some of Meta's internal projects. Google has since limited other clients too — and signed a deal to rent additional cloud capacity from Elon Musk's SpaceX.

Microsoft expands its in-house MAI model lineup

Microsoft AI is filling out the MAI family — including the MAI-Thinking-1 reasoning model and MAI-Code-1-Flash for coding — part of a clear push to lower its dependence on OpenAI and cut token costs for developers building on Azure.

South Korea unveils a sweeping chip-and-AI plan

Seoul announced a major semiconductor and AI investment package centered on Samsung and SK Hynix — new fabs, expanded high-bandwidth memory production, AI data centers and robotics — aimed at locking in Korea's place in the global AI chip supply chain.

8090 raises $135M Series A; Chamath takes the CEO seat

AI software-factory startup 8090 closed a $135M round led by Salesforce Ventures to scale its platform. Investor Chamath Palihapitiya confirmed a return to full-time operating, stepping in directly as the company's CEO.
