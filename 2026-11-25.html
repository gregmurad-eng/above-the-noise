Lead Story

Anthropic ships Claude Opus 4.5 — and the real feature is the dial, not the benchmark

Anthropic released Claude Opus 4.5 on Monday 24 November — — Anthropic[1]. The topline is a benchmark victory: Opus 4.5 is the first model to clear 80% on SWE-bench Verified, beating Google's Gemini 3 Pro and OpenAI's GPT-5.1 on the lab's own agentic-coding comparisons[2]. Bloomberg leads with the office-work angle; CNBC frames it as the third major Anthropic release in two months and notes the $350B valuation backdrop[3].

Those are the headlines. The news for someone building on the API is lower in the post. Opus 4.5 introduces a new parameter called effort — three settings, low / medium / high — that lets the caller trade reasoning depth against output-token spend on a per-request basis. Anthropic claims that at effort=medium, Opus 4.5 matches Sonnet 4.5's intelligence quality at roughly 76% fewer output tokens; at effort=high it pushes the benchmark numbers but spends more[1]. Pricing drops to $5 input / $25 output per million tokens — the first time Anthropic has lowered Opus prices, and a two-thirds cut on input against the previous flagship[1].

Rolled in on the same day: Claude for Chrome is now available to all Max users, Claude for Excel opens up to Max, Team, and Enterprise, and the Claude app gets an "endless chat" mode that silently compresses context when the window fills[2]. The Chrome browser extension and the Excel add-in have been in a limited pilot since the summer; this is the first time either has been in general distribution across paid tiers.

What matters about effort is the shape of the primitive, not Anthropic's marketing. OpenAI's reasoning models already expose a reasoning-effort field, but it is tuned around a specific family. Opus 4.5 is making the compute dial a first-class product surface on a flagship model intended for agentic work — the kind of job where a single run can burn through a thousand tool calls and where marginal token spend is the actual bill. A knob that advertises "same answer, fewer tokens" is a knob every finance review of an agent-heavy product is going to ask about by next month.

The corollary is unflattering for anyone shipping on a fixed model spec. If your agent framework pins a single reasoning intensity by default, you are now a benchmark hostage in the other direction — paying for maximum thought on trivial steps. The teams who get ahead on cost in December will be the ones who treat effort as a planner-level decision, not a global default.

So what: if you run anything agentic on Claude, add an effort column to your request logs this week, benchmark your p95 task at low vs medium, and set a per-step default — treating this as one global setting is the expensive mistake of December.

On 23 November Anthropic launched Claude Ship, a set of three agents: Code Review (GA), PR Steward (beta), and Claude Approvals (beta). Together they can take a GitHub pull request through review, automated fixes, and merge approval under natural-language policy rules for what escalates to a human[5].

So what: this is the first frontier lab offering agent sign-off as a product, not a feature — write the escalation rule for your riskiest repo (anything touching auth, payments, migrations) before you turn Approvals on, or you will discover the policy by having a bad merge roll out.
