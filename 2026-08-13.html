Lead Story

Anthropic quietly turns on Claude watermarking — worldwide, not just for EU customers

— Anthropic support docs / TechCrunch / Fortune, Aug 11

Anthropic began embedding an invisible, machine-readable mark in Claude’s text output starting with models released on or after August 2, 2026, and confirmed the roll-out on August 11[1]. The trigger is the EU AI Act Article 50 transparency clock — the same one that started ticking last week — but the enforcement choice is the real story: the watermark applies to every Claude call on the platform, API, Claude Code, Claude Cowork, and the Bedrock / Google Cloud / Microsoft Foundry passthroughs, regardless of where the caller sits[1]. There is no US carve-out.

Two things distinguish this from earlier “we’ll watermark someday” announcements. First, it is text, not just images — the harder, unsolved half of the provenance problem[2]. Anthropic’s docs say the signal is designed to survive copy-paste and, in their words, “may survive some editing too,” while conceding it can be stripped by format conversion, re-saving, screenshots, or paraphrase-through-another-model[1]. Second, it establishes an industry precedent: an American lab is applying an EU-driven transparency rule globally rather than geo-fencing it, because operating two parallel Claudes is more expensive than shipping the strictest one everywhere[3]. This is the Brussels Effect landing on a foundation model.

The gap the mark leaves is important to name plainly. It proves a specific piece of text passed through Claude; it does not prove authorship, does not resist a determined launderer, and does not extend to any model that shipped before August 2. TechCrunch and Fortune both quote Anthropic conceding the tool is one line of defense against “AI slop” and impersonation, not a solution[2][3]. What it does do is give a downstream detector — a school, a publisher, a courtroom, a rival lab — a positive signal to look for. It moves “was this Claude?” from vibes into a yes/no with a defined false-negative rate.

Corroboration: Anthropic’s own support page is the primary source of record[1]; TechCrunch[2] and Fortune[3] independently confirm the August 11 announcement, the EU trigger, and the global scope. Two tier-1 outlets on top of a primary doc — this one clears the bar.

So what: if your product pipes Claude output into anything downstream — a CMS, a document generator, an eval harness, a search index — assume the mark is there and audit the transformations you apply, because a format-conversion step that quietly strips it moves you from “correctly marked as AI” to “laundering AI content” without changing a line of your code.

SpaceXAI ships Grok 4.6 with a 500k context window at $2/$6 per million

— SpaceXAI docs / SiliconANGLE, Aug 12

Grok 4.6 shipped August 12 on the SpaceXAI API, Grok Build, Cursor, and via OpenRouter, Vercel, and Cloudflare[9]. It is a post-training upgrade over Grok 4.5, not a bigger base — SpaceXAI held the foundation constant and spent the delta on longer supplemental training, regenerated fine-tuning trajectories, and reinforcement learning in agentic environments[10]. Sticker is $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens below 200k prompt tokens, doubling to $4/$12 above[9]; SpaceXAI ties it with GPT-5.6 Sol Max at 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index.

So what: if you run a nightly agent that spills over 200k input tokens on retries, the price tier flips silently — audit whether your job actually needs the full context or whether a summariser at 180k saves half the bill.

CISA adds Progress Kemp LoadMaster pre-auth RCE (CVE-2026-8037, CVSS 9.6) to KEV

— CISA / The Hacker News, Aug 7

CISA added CVE-2026-8037 — an unauthenticated command injection in Progress Kemp LoadMaster — to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 7, with a federal patch deadline of August 10[11]. A single crafted HTTP request to /accessv2 executes shell commands as root; watchTowr Labs traced the root cause to a broken escape_quotes() input sanitiser[12]. Telemetry shows 792 exploitation attempts across 65 IPs in 41 days, most recent activity August 4.

So what: LoadMaster boxes sit at the edge and often front internal AI stacks — if yours does, an /accessv2 access-log grep and a version check are ten minutes; skipping them because “the federal deadline already passed” is exactly the read attackers rely on.
