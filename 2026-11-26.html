Lead Story

US Entity-Lists Megaspeed and Alibaba's Singapore unit; Nvidia and AMD sell off — CNBC[1]

Commerce / Bureau of Industry and Security · acted 2026-11-23, effective 2026-11-25

The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security added seven firms to the Entity List on Monday 23 November, with restrictions taking effect Tuesday 25 November under a presumption-of-denial licensing standard. Two names on the list reshape the AI compute supply chain. The first is Megaspeed International, a Singapore-based Nvidia cloud partner that had been under BIS investigation since 2024 for diverting advanced GPUs into the People's Republic of China.[1] The second is Apex Enterprise Solutions, a Singapore subsidiary of Alibaba. Alibaba disputed the designation on Tuesday.[2]

The listings follow — and operationally tighten — the BIS interim final rule published on 19 November that restricts remote access to US-origin advanced AI chips when that access is routed through overseas data centres in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil.[3] Taken together, the two actions name the jurisdictions that regulators believe have been doing the quiet work of chip diversion, and they attach a specific sanctions instrument to a named cloud partner inside one of them.

The market read was unambiguous. Nvidia closed down 5.9 per cent on Tuesday 24 November; AMD fell 8.9 per cent.[1] This is not a geopolitical headline that will fade by Friday. It is a structural change to how American silicon reaches an inference customer anywhere between California and Shenzhen, and it names a specific vendor that a non-trivial share of Western compute buyers had been renting from.

The shift from "export rule on chips" to "Entity-Listed cloud partner" is the one builders should internalise. Until this week, the controlled object was a GPU crossing a border. Now the controlled object is a named cloud tenant that might route your inference through a jurisdiction on BIS's new covered list. The compliance surface has moved from procurement to runtime.

If you lease GPU capacity, audit your provider's region map this week: any inference routed through Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, or Brazil now carries remote-access licence exposure, and a named Nvidia cloud partner in that corridor is Entity-Listed. Price in a contract repricing event inside Q1 2027 for anything mission-critical.

Today's Signal

The compute supply chain is becoming an enforcement surface, and the inference stack is becoming self-rewritable.

Three different vectors converged this week. BIS named a specific Nvidia cloud partner inside Singapore and attached a license-denial presumption to remote access through five covered jurisdictions. The FCC blessed up to a million orbital AI compute satellites. And Anthropic used its own agents to rewrite the front door of its API in Rust, publishing a 70/80/20 per cent cost disclosure as a number rather than a claim. The physical location, the legal location, and the per-request cost of inference are all moving at the same time — and all three are now reportable facts instead of handwaved assumptions. Build for a world in which someone asks you to produce those three numbers for your own stack by the end of 2027.
