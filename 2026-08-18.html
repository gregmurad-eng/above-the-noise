Lead Story

Nvidia posts a $105B backstop for OpenAI's Ohio compute campus, in an SEC filing — Nvidia

Nvidia disclosed in an 8-K yesterday that it has entered into residual-value guaranties with SB Energy covering roughly 4.25 gigawatts of IT load at the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus in Pike County, Ohio, with cumulative exposure capped at $105 billion.[1][2] An affiliate of OpenAI Group PBC signs a 20-year lease as the exclusive tenant; SB Energy builds, owns, and operates the site; Nvidia also puts $1.5 billion of equity into SB Energy and takes an option on another 3.75 GW, for a total of 8 GW.[1][3]

Read the filing carefully. The $105B is a conditional ceiling, not cash Nvidia is handing over now: it is the maximum amount Nvidia could be called on to pay the lessor if OpenAI's lease payments fail. OpenAI has agreed to reimburse and indemnify Nvidia for anything Nvidia actually pays.[2] The structure is closer to a lease guaranty from a strategic supplier than to project financing.

Two things matter for anyone building on top of this stack.

First, Nvidia has now put its balance sheet, publicly and in a securities filing, behind a customer's long-dated infrastructure lease. That is a different instrument from the $500B Nvidia-anchored capital framework Huang and Larry Fink walked through on CNBC on August 10;[4] this is Nvidia agreeing to be the buyer of last resort of a specific 20-year OpenAI lease. Huang told CNBC yesterday that this is not circular financing — "OpenAI will pay the lease" — and floated a $600B compute opportunity across OpenAI's plans through 2030, with roughly 1.5 million Nvidia GPUs and $150-200B of revenue per hardware refresh at the Ohio site alone.[5] Whatever the label, the risk-transfer is real: if OpenAI ever cannot pay, Nvidia has agreed to.

Second, the ownership diagram is upside-down relative to the pre-2024 hyperscaler model. OpenAI is the tenant, not the owner. Nvidia is the credit prop, not the operator. SB Energy — an infrastructure-fund-backed developer, not a cloud — owns and operates the racks. Compare that to Anthropic's Theseus JV with Macquarie and GIC from ten days ago and the pattern is legible: frontier labs no longer buy their own data centres; they anchor other people's, and their chip supplier posts the collateral.

Third, community and workforce sweeteners are now table stakes for siting an 8 GW AI campus in a U.S. county. The combined package here is over $160M — an $80M SB Energy fund topped up by $40M from OpenAI, plus $84M in Codex credits for roughly 844,000 Ohio college and technical students in the 2026-27 academic year.[3] Expect the next few of these to lead with the local-benefits number, not the gigawatts.

if your product depends on OpenAI API availability past 2028, the compute-supply risk you were pricing has moved: the physical site now exists on paper and is anchored on Nvidia's credit, so the binding constraint on your roadmap is unlikely to be OpenAI capacity — it is more likely to be OpenAI pricing, since paying a 20-year lease at scale is a cost pass-through, not a fixed cost the lab absorbs.

The Briefs

Anthropic tells investors Q2 revenue topped $11.5B, first-ever positive operating income — CNBC

Anthropic disclosed to prospective IPO investors that preliminary Q2 2026 revenue exceeded $11.5 billion, up from $787M in Q2 2025 and $4.73B in Q1 2026, with positive adjusted operating income for the quarter.[6][7] Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and JPMorgan are leading the process; the confidential S-1 was filed with the SEC in June and an offering is possible as early as fall. The number matters because it is the first quarter in which a pure-play frontier lab has printed operating profit — the "AI is a real business" claim now has a P&L line to point at.

if your budget model for Claude assumes token prices keep falling because Anthropic is subsidising traffic to grow into an IPO, invert the assumption — a lab that just hit operating profit will defend margin through the IPO window, not tank it.

Google shuts down three Imagen 4 API endpoints today — Firebase docs

Today, August 18 (following the August 17 deprecation date in Google's docs), the imagen-4.0-generate-001, imagen-4.0-fast-generate-001, and imagen-4.0-ultra-generate-001 endpoints stop responding on the Gemini API; migration is to gemini-2.5-flash-image (Nano Banana) via generate_content, not generate_images.[8][9] It is a hard break, not a warning — the client method is gone and the response shape is content parts instead of an image object. If you shipped an image pipeline against Imagen 4 and did not migrate, it fails silently at the SDK boundary today.

if you do not know for certain that every image-generating code path in your product moved off Imagen 4, ship a synthetic canary that hits the endpoint from prod every hour and pages you on 404 — this is the exact class of deprecation that shows up as a customer support ticket three weeks after it broke.

AI-video platform Higgsfield raises $400M Series B at $5.4B — Higgsfield press release

Higgsfield closed a $400M Series B led by DST Global, quadrupling its $1.3B Series A valuation from December.[10][11] The number that actually matters is the revenue trajectory: ARR is $700M this month, up from $20M a year ago, with 390 of the Fortune 500 as customers and a 42x jump in agentic-product users in three months. This is the first AI-video pureplay to demonstrably clear enterprise revenue at scale rather than living on creator-tier subscriptions.

if your product has a place for generative video and you have been waiting for the market to settle before picking a vendor, the market did not settle — one vendor just pulled ahead on the enterprise revenue metric that decides which video APIs get maintained past 2027, so pick your bet or accept the switching cost later.

Ryanair signs five-year Google Cloud + Gemini deal covering 35,000 staff — Google Cloud

Europe's largest airline picked Google Cloud as a second hyperscaler alongside its existing AWS estate, deploying Google Workspace, Gemini Enterprise, and DeepMind's AlphaEvolve and WeatherNext models against crew scheduling, fleet operations, and maintenance planning.[12][13] Terms undisclosed. The signal is dual-cloud as an AI risk-management posture: after the 2024-25 airline IT outage run, "one AI vendor" is no longer an acceptable single point of failure for operational carriers.

if you sell to enterprises with real operational blast radius (airlines, banks, utilities), the buyer conversation this quarter is going to include "how does this run against a second model provider" as a first-class requirement — bake a swappable-provider abstraction in before your first customer asks for it in a security review.

Dense 27B-parameter multimodal open-weights model under Apache 2.0, 262K native context (extensible to 1M via YaRN), native image+video input, ~16-17 GB VRAM to run.[14] Passed 3 million downloads in its first weekend. Simon Willison's evaluation, published yesterday, put it at 52 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — competitive with much larger frontier models — while noting a strong tendency to over-reason on trivial prompts.[15]

a 27B multimodal model that scores in the frontier band and fits on one 24 GB workstation card meaningfully lowers the bar for prototyping vision-language agents locally; if your current dev loop involves waiting on API credits to iterate on prompts, put this in your loop first and reserve the API call for the eval.

Voices

Jensen Huang, founder and CEO of NVIDIA, in the company's press release yesterday announcing the OpenAI Ohio deal:[1]

"AI is becoming infrastructure – the foundation for intelligence in every industry – and land, power and shell have become vital in the age of AI."

Coming from the CEO whose company just committed to backstop $105B of a 20-year data-centre lease, this line is doing more work than it looks. Two years ago Nvidia sold GPUs; a year ago it sold reference designs; this quarter it is selling the position that AI capacity is a public-utility-shaped asset that needs public-utility-shaped financing, and that Nvidia belongs on the credit side of that structure as well as the supply side. That is the framing every other frontier chip vendor and every other hyperscaler will now have to answer to, and it changes what the word "vendor" means in an AI supply chain.

if your procurement narrative for AI infrastructure has been "buy compute like SaaS", update it — the buy-side conversation for anything past 1 GW is now shaped like a utility interconnect deal, and the vocabulary of that conversation (residual value, take-or-pay, land-power-shell, anchor tenant) is what your finance counterparts will need to hear from you by year-end.

Today's Signal

The compute question for AI has stopped being "who owns the racks" and started being "whose balance sheet is on the lease." Nvidia posting a $105B residual-value guaranty in an 8-K for a customer's 20-year Ohio lease is a category shift, not a scale-up of what it did before. It sits alongside Anthropic anchoring the Theseus JV as a tenant, SB Energy owning and operating the site as an infrastructure-fund play, and Stripe paying $7B for the routing layer that decides which model each API call actually hits. Four different companies, four different layers, one consistent shape: the frontier labs are becoming the tenant and the payer, and everyone else — chip vendor, developer, payments company — is claiming a durable slice of the plumbing around them. The interesting question for the rest of 2026 is not which lab ships the next frontier model. It is which non-lab company posts the next multi-billion-dollar guaranty, and against whose lease.
