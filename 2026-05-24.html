Lead Story

White House Scraps AI Executive Order Hours Before Signing

A voluntary 90-day pre-launch review framework for frontier models was pulled at the last minute. No explanation was given.

On May 21st, hours before a scheduled signing ceremony, the White House quietly withdrew an executive order that would have established a voluntary 90-day pre-launch review framework for frontier AI models. No public explanation followed.

The order wasn't binding — it was voluntary and review-focused, not a hard restriction. The fact that it was pulled anyway signals something about where the current administration's calculus sits on the spectrum between economic competitiveness and safety guardrails.

The practical near-term effect is minimal. Major labs were already cooperating informally with government reviewers. But the symbolic effect is loud: the US remains without any formal pre-deployment review mechanism for the most capable AI systems, even voluntary ones. That's a meaningful contrast with the EU AI Act's tiered risk framework, which is now fully in force.

The move likely emboldens labs to move faster on release cycles. Whether that's good or bad depends on who you ask — but it does mean the next few months of model releases will be less encumbered than they might have been under even a soft review structure.

The Briefs

Anthropic Hits $900B Valuation, Reports First Quarterly Profit

Anthropic is closing in on a $900 billion valuation and reported its first quarterly operating profit. An IPO is reportedly being prepared. Separately, Anthropic and the Gates Foundation announced a $200 million, four-year partnership to develop AI tools for healthcare, education, agriculture, and economic development in underserved regions.

Google Search Is Getting Persistent Background Agents

Google announced that "information agents" — AI agents that operate 24/7 in the background, monitoring for updates on topics you care about — are coming to Search this summer. First access goes to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. It's the clearest signal yet that the next phase of search isn't queries, it's subscriptions to ongoing intelligence.

GitHub Supply Chain Attack: Thousands of Repos Exposed

GitHub disclosed that attackers compromised an employee device via a malicious version of the Nx Console VS Code extension, gaining access to thousands of internal repositories. It's a textbook supply-chain attack — the kind the security community has been warning about since the xz utils incident. If you use VS Code extensions in your workflow, now is a good time to audit your installed list.

Telegram Adding AI Bots That Can Read and Reply to Messages

Telegram is introducing assistant bots capable of reading, filtering, and replying to messages based on user-defined permissions. For solo builders using Telegram for ops or community (like OpenClaw-style setups), this opens up interesting automation possibilities — but also raises questions about where that data goes.

Gemini 3.5 Flash: One Week In

A week after general availability, the consensus from developers is: the speed claims hold up, the reasoning is genuinely competitive, and the $1.50/1M input pricing is aggressive enough to pressure OpenAI's GPT-4o mini tier. Early benchmarks on coding tasks are favorable. The 1M context window is the sleeper feature.

Today's Signal

The pulled executive order and the GitHub supply chain attack landed the same week. One reminds you that governance moves slower than the technology. The other reminds you that the attack surface grows with every dev tool you add. For solo builders: your VS Code extension list is part of your security posture. Treat it like one.
