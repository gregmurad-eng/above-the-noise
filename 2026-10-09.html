Lead Story

Anthropic ships Haiku 5.5 at $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens — and quietly rewrites the small-model economics around cache[1] — Anthropic

Published 7 October 2026 · corroborated by SiliconANGLE[2]

Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5 on 7 October, priced at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens for prompts up to 100,000 tokens — a roughly 90% cut from Haiku 4.5 at the equivalent tier — and lifted the context window from 200K to one million, with 128K output tokens[1]. The model is multimodal, carries adjustable thinking by default for the first time in the Haiku line, and ships simultaneously on the Anthropic API, AWS Bedrock, Google Vertex AI and Azure AI Foundry.

The headline price matches the floor OpenAI set on 22 September with GPT-6 Luna, which this newsletter noted was the signal that the fast-small tier had become a commodity line. The more consequential move in the same announcement is the one Anthropic buried below the fold: Sonnet 5.5 cache-read pricing is now $0.10 per million tokens — half what it was[2]. Anthropic puts the net effect at a roughly 20% drop in cost on cache-heavy agentic workloads, which, in the kinds of production systems that reuse large context prefixes across dozens or hundreds of calls, is the number that actually shows up on the invoice.

Independent reporting from SiliconANGLE flags benchmark comparisons Anthropic is willing to put on paper: Haiku 5.5 against GPT-6 Luna, 72.4% vs 48.9% on OSWorld 2.1 (agent tasks) and 39.2% vs 16.4% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (coding)[2]. Benchmark numbers from a lab talking about its own model deserve the usual skepticism; the pricing numbers do not — they are legally binding on invoices.

Two things to notice. First, the sticker price has stopped moving. Both frontier small-model vendors are now at $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens; this is a floor, and it holds across tokenizers that disagree by 20-30% on the same prompt. Second, the real competition is now cache economics, prompt reuse, and context length at that price — Haiku 5.5's 1M window at ten cents per million input tokens is a different product than Haiku 4.5 at the same number. A long-context cache hit at $0.01/M (Anthropic's cache-read rate for Haiku 5.5) is, in practice, a nearly free re-read of the whole working set. The economic argument against building agent memory as context rather than retrieval just got harder to make.

The thing the headline misses: Anthropic is pitching this as "the cost of a world-class model has fallen 90%" — which is roughly the pattern Haiku has followed every generation. But the delta that matters is not Haiku-to-Haiku. It is that the second-most-expensive thing in a serious agent today — reused prompt prefixes under long context — just got cut in half on Anthropic's mid-tier model on the same day. That is the economic signal.

If you run agent systems with reused context prefixes on Sonnet, rerun your cost model this week with cache-read at $0.10/M, not $0.20 — the breakeven for storing more in context versus retrieval has moved against retrieval, and the longer your working set the more it moves.

Today's Signal

The pricing fight at the small/fast tier is over — $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens is the commodity floor, and both Anthropic and OpenAI are camped on it. What differentiates frontier small models from here is cache economics, context length at that floor, and how cheap prompt reuse becomes under long context.
