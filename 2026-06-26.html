Lead Story

OpenAI's GPT-5.5-Cyber Tops the Charts While Anthropic Sits Benched

OpenAI this week shipped the full version of GPT-5.5-Cyber, a model purpose-built for defensive security work, and it landed with a number designed to make headlines: 85.6% on CyberGym, the UC Berkeley benchmark that asks an AI agent to reproduce 1,507 known vulnerabilities across 188 open-source projects. That edges out base GPT-5.5 at 81.8%, and — pointedly — beats Anthropic's Mythos 5 at 83.8% and Claude Opus 4 at 73.1%. (Axios)

The model is engineered for the entire defensive loop, not a party trick. It reads large codebases, traces attack paths, validates likely vulnerabilities inside a controlled sandbox, writes patches, and tests those patches before handing them to a human reviewer. Access is gated to vetted security firms and researchers, and OpenAI is wrapping it in a new Daybreak Cyber Partner Program that lets approved vendors embed GPT-5.5 "with Trusted Access for Cyber" directly into the products they sell. (The Decoder)

The subtext is the real story. Earlier this month the U.S. government issued an export-control directive that barred foreign nationals from accessing Anthropic's top-tier Mythos and Fable models on national-security grounds. So the most capable cyber model from one lab is benched for much of the world, and OpenAI just rolled a competitive — and more permissively licensed — alternative into the gap. (ForkLog)

Why it matters for builders: Benchmark bragging rights are noise; the access model is the signal. A frontier cyber capability that you can actually license and embed beats a marginally stronger one you can't touch. If you're building security tooling, the question isn't "which model scores higher" but "which model can I ship inside my product without tripping an export wall." Right now that answer points toward OpenAI's Daybreak track — and that distribution advantage, not the 1.8 benchmark points, is what reshapes the market. Watch whether the gating stays narrow or whether "vetted access" quietly becomes the default posture for every frontier capability that touches offense.

The Briefs

A second Transformer author walks out of DeepMind

Noam Shazeer — co-author of the 2017 "Attention Is All You Need" paper that underpins essentially every modern model — is leaving Google DeepMind for OpenAI. It follows news that Nobel laureate and AlphaFold creator John Jumper is departing DeepMind for Anthropic. The talent reshuffle at the top is accelerating. (BuildFastWithAI)

Micron's revenue quadruples on the memory crunch

Micron posted fiscal Q3 revenue of $41.46 billion, up roughly fourfold year over year, driven by an acute AI memory shortage that's reshaping hardware economics. The bottleneck for AI buildout is increasingly memory, not just GPUs. (Tech Startups)

Qualcomm buys Modular for ~$4B

Qualcomm is acquiring AI-infra startup Modular in an all-stock deal worth nearly $4 billion, picking up a software stack for running models across heterogeneous processors. The mobile-chip giant wants a credible cross-hardware inference story. (Tech Startups)

A vision-language model is now running in orbit

Loft Orbital's YAM-9 satellite is running Google's Gemma 3 on-board — the first deployment of a vision-language model in space. Ground teams can ask it natural-language questions about what it sees, processing imagery locally instead of beaming raw data back to Earth. (unrot.co)

ChatGPT slips below 50% share

ChatGPT's market share fell to 46.4% by late May — its first time under half — as Gemini climbed to 27.7% and Claude reached 10.3%. The single-assistant era is fading; assume your users live across several. (The AI Track)
