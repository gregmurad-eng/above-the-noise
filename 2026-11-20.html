Lead Story

Anthropic begins trading on Nasdaq at a $1.8 trillion market cap — Anthropic[1]

Primary IPO pricing notice and S-1/A final prospectus; Nasdaq debut 19 November 2026 under symbol ANT.

At 11:48 a.m. ET on Thursday 19 November, Anthropic shares opened on the Nasdaq Global Select Market at $111.50, a 34% pop above the $83.00 IPO price set the night before.[2] By the close, ANT had given most of that back and settled at $97.17 — still +17% on the day, but a 13% reversal intraday — leaving Anthropic with a fully diluted market cap of roughly $1.8 trillion on 980 million shares sold at $83, raising $81.3 billion.[1][2] The raise edges out SpaceX's June listing to become the largest IPO on record.[3]

The number that matters for builders is not the headline cap, it is the shape of the day. Private-round investors paid prices that only made sense if compute spend compounded indefinitely into gross margin. The public-market bid said something different: a 34% pop, then a steady drift down to +17%, means institutional investors bought the capability story but discounted the capex story. Anthropic's S-1/A disclosed four distinct silicon stacks (AWS Trainium, Google TPU, Nvidia GPU, AMD GPU) and a seven-company cornerstone coalition — AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, TSMC — buying $26 billion of Class N shares.[1] That diversification is now the template every peer has to match if it wants comparable buyer demand, which is exactly what the 2026-11-16 call in the Ledger tracks.

Dario Amodei sat with Andrew Ross Sorkin on CNBC the same morning and did not duck the question. On pacing: “you could drive at 200 miles an hour. Maybe let's only drive at 90 miles an hour, spend time figuring out how to do that safely.”[4] On compute commitments reaching $900 billion: “the returns to that are so high that we are willing to put in place these very large compute spends because, historically, we have seen over and over again that it pays off.”[4] The two sentences sit in tension, and that tension is now a public filing fact, not a private one. Model-card family; Neon released 18–19 November 2026 in Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, the Gemini app, and Antigravity.

The package: a tiered emergency-shutdown authority (the headline “kill switch” is multi-layered, not one button), mandatory ongoing independent verification of frontier labs, incident-reporting timelines cut from 15 days to 48 hours (24 hours for imminent catastrophic risks), near-miss and model-weight-theft reporting channels, and broader whistleblower protection.[12] Newsom says he is working with legislators to turn it into bills.

CISA adds LiteLLM CVE-2026-40217 to Known Exploited Vulnerabilities — GitHub Advisory Database (and CISA KEV)[13]

KEV entry dated 18 November 2026; federal remediation deadline 2 December 2026.

The flaw: LiteLLM's custom-guardrail test endpoint POST /guardrails/test_custom_code runs user-submitted Python inside a hand-rolled regex deny-list sandbox, bypassable via bytecode rewriting, giving remote attackers full code execution on the gateway host. Affects LiteLLM >= 1.81.8, < 1.83.10; fixed in 1.83.11. CISA added it to KEV with evidence of real attacks.[14]

So what: if you run LiteLLM as a self-hosted proxy for virtual keys, your gateway host is the identity for every downstream model — patch to 1.83.11 today, and audit any custom guardrails you shipped against the test endpoint. The guardrail code path is the softest target in the LLM-proxy category right now.
