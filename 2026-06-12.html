Lead Story

SpaceX prices the largest IPO in history at a $1.75 trillion valuation

SpaceX priced its initial public offering at $135 per share on Thursday, raising roughly $75 billion and landing a valuation near $1.75 trillion — instantly one of the ten most valuable public companies on Earth. The deal dwarfs the prior record, Saudi Aramco's $35.4 billion listing in 2019, and the stock begins trading today on the Nasdaq under the ticker SPCX.

The company going public is not the SpaceX of a few years ago. After its merger with xAI, the entity now spans three segments: Starlink satellite internet, launch services, and the xAI model business. Investors are effectively buying a bet that orbital infrastructure and frontier AI belong on the same balance sheet — that the same vertical-integration playbook that drove launch costs down can be aimed at compute and model training.

Wall Street is split. Bulls call the combined launch-plus-AI franchise a generational "holy grail" with no clean comparable. Skeptics point at the math: at $135 a share the multiple bakes in years of flawless Starlink expansion and an xAI unit that still burns cash to keep pace with OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic. Analysts remain divided on whether the price reflects fundamentals or the Musk premium. MSCI has already confirmed it will fast-track SPCX into its World and ACWI indices, forcing index funds to buy in within days regardless of the debate.

For people building on top of this stack, the relevant question is not the share price — it's concentration. A single newly-public giant now controls a dominant slice of low-earth-orbit connectivity and operates a frontier model lab. That is enormous distribution leverage if you build in their ecosystem, and a single point of failure if you depend on it.

The story underneath the headline number is consolidation. We're watching launch, connectivity, and AI fold into one publicly-traded entity. For solo builders the lesson is the same as always: ride the platforms, but never let one vendor own your whole supply chain.
