Lead Story

The agent-era stack landed its rails this week

While the headline chair at Nvidia sat empty[1], the plumbing underneath AI agents quietly hardened. Over the last seventy-two hours, five distinct layers of the "agents that do things" stack moved from research posture to shipped product. They did not ship as a coordinated package — nobody runs that playbook — but the week reads like one, because every piece answers the same question: what does an agent need in order to be trusted to act?

Start at the top of the stack. On 13 November OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, in partnership with American Express, Mastercard, and Visa[2]. Dots — OpenAI's always-on agents — can now complete purchases on a Pro subscriber's behalf without ever seeing the real card number; the wallet issues a payment token and requires a security check before each transaction. The three card networks co-signed it, which is what the headline is actually about. Agentic payments stopped being an aspiration slide the day Visa and Mastercard put their names on one.

Beneath payments sits the agent's web surface. Cloudflare, the same day, open-sourced Kitesurf[3], the browser engine it built for agents instead of shipping a full copy of Chrome. It is written in Rust, compiles to WebAssembly, runs entirely inside Cloudflare Workers isolates, and renders pages without a Chromium binary. Apache 2.0, inspectable, deployable to your own Cloudflare account. The pitch is not "faster Chrome" — it is a smaller, auditable surface for an agent to touch the web with.

Beneath the browser sits the compute. On 9 November Google added TPU 8i Max and TPU 8tx to its eighth-generation lineup[4]. 8i Max is two 8i chips on one package — 768 MB of on-chip SRAM, 17.2 TB/s of HBM bandwidth — explicitly positioned for long-context agentic workloads. 8tx is a confidential-compute variant for regulated and federal workloads. Four new TPU systems in a single calendar year is not a product cadence; it is a bet that long-session agent inference is the next quarter's bottleneck.

Beneath compute sits governance. On 13 November LangChain closed a $300M Series C at a $3B valuation, led by CapitalG[5], with GIC, DFJ Growth, Salesforce Ventures and MongoDB Ventures in. LangSmith — their observe/evaluate/govern plane — now runs across more than 7,000 organizations and processes over a billion agent events a day. CapitalG writing the biggest check in that round is Alphabet buying a seat at the agent-governance table it does not yet own with Gemini.

And beneath governance sits the thing enterprises actually pay for: did any of this work? On 12 November, Nebuly raised a $21M Series A led by Planven to measure exactly that[6] — AI-influenced revenue, churn risk, upsell signals, deployment ROI — across Claude, ChatGPT and Copilot, with Oura, Iveco and Zeiss named as paying customers. It is the smallest round of the five, and the one that tells you the category is now mature enough for the CFO question.

Five layers, five shipped products, one week. The question for a builder is no longer "can I build an agent that does things" — it is "whose rails am I standing on when it does."

So what: if you are shipping an agent that touches the web, holds a budget, or lives inside an enterprise, write down your dependency on each of these five layers this week. ChatGPT Wallet locks you into dots and OpenAI's security-check UX; Kitesurf frees you from a Chromium footprint but ties you to Cloudflare Workers; LangSmith is becoming the audit log regulators will ask to see; Nebuly is the number your CFO will quote at renewal. Decide which rail you want to be on before your competitors choose for you.

The Financial Times separately reports the board approached Arm CEO Rene Haas; Nvidia says no candidate has been formally engaged. If you are on a 2027 GPU allocation plan with Nvidia, assume your account map changes once a permanent CEO lands — write the next review assuming a new relationship, not a continuation.

Anthropic launches Claude for Manufacturing with Infor as preferred ERP — Anthropic

On 12 November, mid-IPO-roadshow, Anthropic shipped Claude for Manufacturing — a plugin with 17 skills for quoting, quality investigation, production reporting, maintenance diagnosis, sourcing and design, connected to Autodesk Fusion, Infor, Microsoft 365 and Oracle NetSuite[8]. Infor announced the same day that it has designated Claude its preferred AI assistant across its Industry AI products[9]. Buyers, planners and CS reps can now ask operational questions in plain language and execute approved transactions — PO approvals, stock reallocation, production-line shifts — without leaving the Claude conversation.

So what: Anthropic is pricing the IPO and shipping a vertical ERP integration on the same tape. If your product sits next to an Infor deployment, expect your buyer to have a Claude budget line by Q1 — write the integration brief now rather than after the deal closes.

Thinking Machines opens Inkling 2 Small weights, 594B MoE under Apache 2.0 — Thinking Machines Lab

On 12 November, a week after the hosted launch via Tinker, Thinking Machines Lab published the full open weights of Inkling 2 Small on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0, in two checkpoints (NVFP4 and MXFP8) totalling roughly 393.5 GB[10]. It is a 594B-total-parameter Mixture-of-Experts with 17B active, 1M context, and image-input support. The release followed external safety testing and red-teaming per the original announcement commitment.

So what: open-weight frontier releases in this class let you move an evaluation or batch-scoring job off hosted APIs for less than one GPU-month of your time. If you have an inference bill with a frontier lab of more than $50K a month on a non-interactive workload, benchmark Inkling 2 Small against your current stack before renewal.

SMIC: AI chip supply will stay tight through 2027 — CASRAI (summarising SMIC co-CEO comments)

SMIC's co-CEO said on 13 November that AI-related chip supply will stay tight through 2027[11]. The signal echoes Microsoft CFO Amy Hood's 28 October earnings call saying third-party AI chip supply delays will push some capacity into H1 2027[12], and TSMC's 2026 agentic-chip demand commentary[13]. Nvidia disclosed $279B of supply commitments in Q2, primarily memory. Supply, not demand, is the binding constraint going into next year.

So what: if your 2027 roadmap assumes available GPU capacity on-demand through a hyperscaler, the earnings calls just confirmed that assumption is wrong. Reserve or commit now, or move the workload to a model that fits inside a smaller silicon envelope.

Gemini 4 Hydrogen — Google (12 November, pricing landed)

$1.50 per million input tokens, $7.50 per million output; 2.5× faster task completion than Flash at default thinking; 57% on Terminal-Bench 4.1; free tier in the Gemini app and Search[14]. Positioned as Google's fast, disciplined execution model. Below GPT-6 Sol's $2 input tier — but as Simon Willison flagged on 12 November, still 15× Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna at $0.10/$0.50, so the floor it resets is the mid-frontier tier, not the cheap tier.

So what: for any new product requiring Gemini-native tool use or Android distribution, Hydrogen is now the sensible default. For everything else, the long-tail pricing floor is still Haiku 5.5 or GPT-6 Luna, and switching to Hydrogen for cost alone is a losing math.

TPU 8i Max & TPU 8tx — Google (9 November)

Not a model, but the compute aimed at today's models: TPU 8i Max is two 8i chips on one package — 768 MB of on-chip SRAM, 17.2 TB/s HBM bandwidth — built for long-context agentic inference. TPU 8tx builds confidential-compute isolation into hardware, aimed at federal and regulated workloads[4]. Availability dates and pricing not yet announced.

So what: if you are running a Gemini-family workload with greater than 500K tokens of context per request, add 8i Max to your Q1 vendor review — Google quoting it as agent-era silicon signals pricing will favour long-session workloads that today get rate-limited.

This edition's Voice is Demis Hassabis, co-founder and CEO of Google DeepMind. Recorded live at the Masters of Scale Summit at the Presidio Theatre in San Francisco in October and published in early November, Hassabis's conversation with Reid Hoffman — "Society is 'not ready' for the AI future", about 55 minutes[15] — is the most senior frontier-lab leader to date giving a named prediction of the near-term failure mode. His framing: "Some kind of incident, I sometimes call it like an oil spill, is going to happen on the current trajectory."[15] He pairs the warning with what DeepMind is doing about it inside the lab — "much more hardened sandboxes" walling off what an agent can reach — a concrete answer to the critique that frontier labs talk about safety abstractly. The best fifteen minutes for a builder start roughly where Hoffman asks what an incident would actually look like.

So what: if you are shipping agents into any environment with real-world side effects, the "hardened sandbox" phrase just became the vocabulary your buyer will expect you to speak. If your product does not have a documented isolation story — what the agent can reach, what it can't, and what a failure contains — write one this week before a customer asks.

Today's Signal

The agentic stack has rails now. A week ago, "agents that act in the world" was still a research posture — this week, the browser (Kitesurf), payments (ChatGPT Wallet), compute (TPU 8i Max), governance (LangChain / LangSmith) and measurement (Nebuly) all shipped in production form. None of these were announced together. All five answer the same question. The next twelve months of competition in this category will not be won by whoever has the smartest model — the model layer is already saturated — but by whoever stands on the right combination of rails. Choose your stack with the same care you once chose a database.
