Lead Story

Mistral ships Le Chonk — a 1-trillion-parameter European open-weights model, pitched directly at the Chinese stack

Mistral released Mistral Large 4[1] — codenamed "Le Chonk" — on 6 October: a 1-trillion-parameter natively-multimodal mixture-of-experts with 49 billion active parameters, trained from scratch over roughly two months on 3,800 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs in Mistral's own European data centres — Mistral AI (primary). The preview is live via Mistral Studio at $1.36 per million input tokens and $4.18 output; full open weights are promised by the end of October after a three-week red-teaming window with developers, cybersecurity leaders and government authorities.

CEO Arthur Mensch unveiled the model at the Ai Everything conference in Abu Dhabi and told Reuters[2] the model "is actually above the Chinese models on certain aspects, including cyber" — Reuters (tier-1). Mistral's own numbers back a careful version of that: 82% on a cyber-vulnerability-reproduction test it says leads open-weight models outside China, 61.7% on DeepSWE v1.1, and a Dense-200 visual-grounding score of 42%, nosing ahead of GPT-6 Astra. On the generalist leaderboards it is still a step behind the frontier — Simon Willison, running his own evaluation on Artificial Analysis[3], places it at 38, just behind DeepSeek 4.1 Flash, and reads the release as "Mistral being back to being maybe about six months behind the frontier" — Simon Willison (practitioner).

The pitch under the pitch is sovereignty. The model was trained end-to-end on EU soil, speaks 160+ languages including every official EU language, and ships with a deployment story that lets governments and regulated enterprises keep inference inside their jurisdiction — CNBC[4] quotes Mensch arguing "the narrative that Europe cannot compete is something that is not true." He pointedly held the launch in the Gulf, not Paris, and spent part of the stage telling the audience that "having very powerful cyber models deployed here in Abu Dhabi in a way where the deployment is fully under Abu Dhabi control is going to be quite important." — The National[5] (tier-1).

Read the week together and the shape is unmistakable. Reflection AI's Beam, announced one day earlier, is a 501B open-weights MoE pitched explicitly as a Western answer to Chinese open models[6]. Google retook benchmark pole position with Gemini 4 Argon on 30 September but is rationing access to vetted cyber teams and paid API customers[7]. The three closed US frontier labs still own the top of the stack. The thing that is new, and that today's news crystallises, is a parallel open-weights lane being drawn — not against OpenAI and Anthropic, but against DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi and GLM — with sovereignty of deployment as the sales line. For a buyer with a data-residency problem, that is suddenly a real market, not a slide.

If you build on open weights, your Oct 27 shortlist just grew by one serious European option — plan an eval run on ML4 against your current Qwen 3.8 or DeepSeek V4 baseline, and if "weights must live in-region" is on a buyer's RFP, Mistral is now the shortest answer.

The Briefs

Sierra and Meta publish the Personal Agent Protocol, an OAuth-based open standard for personal AI agents[8]

Sierra and Meta published a v0.1 spec on 6 October for a shared way for personal agents to authenticate to businesses, declare intent, and carry context across channels, routed via a company's existing site, an MCP/OpenAPI surface, or its own agent. Walmart, Shopify, Stripe, Rocket, Genesys and Instinct are listed as founding partners; Amazon, OpenAI and Anthropic are not — The Next Web (trade).

If your product has a login wall, start treating "signed-in agent" as a first-class caller shape now — the OAuth-with-agent-identity-claims pattern this standardises is what your bot-detection and rate-limit middleware will see traffic from within two quarters, whether you ship a PAP endpoint or not.

Reflection AI unveils Beam, a 501B / 23B-active open-weights MoE pitched at Chinese models[6]

Reflection introduced Beam on 5 October: 501B total parameters, 23B active, pretrained on 23.8T tokens, with waitlisted API access now and full weights, a technical report, and model card promised later this month under Apache 2.0. The company positions it as competitive with GLM-5.2 and approaching Qwen 3.8-Max on coding and agentic tasks "while using three to four times less inference compute" — unite.ai (trade).

A permissively-licensed 23B-active model that approaches Qwen 3.8-Max is a credible self-hosted swap for the Chinese open-weights tier your legal team keeps bouncing — pencil it into the "Apache-2.0 only" column of your model-selection matrix for the Nov 1 re-grade.

Google's Gemini 4 Argon retakes the benchmark lead, but ships behind a gate[7]

Argon posts the highest or tied-highest score in 13 of 19 of Google's comparison benchmarks against GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 — 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, 51.3% on AutomationBench, 91.7% on LVBench — but initial access is limited to vetted cybersecurity teams, Google AI Ultra subscribers, and paid API customers — 9to5Google (trade). The pattern (frontier capability behind a vetted-partner gate) tracks the governance template this newsletter called out in August and has now been re-run by three labs.

If you have an eval harness, point it at Argon's long-horizon tasks (DeepSWE, AutomationBench) before you re-sign a 2027 contract with your current vendor — a 3-5 point real-task lead at frontier is where router rules start getting rewritten.

DeepSeek is finalising ~$12B from Tencent and CATL ahead of an early-2027 IPO[9]

Bloomberg reported on 6 October that DeepSeek is closing on at least 80 billion yuan (~$12B) with Tencent and battery giant CATL among the biggest backers; the lab originally sought 50 billion yuan and signed term sheets could push the final total toward 100 billion. Proceeds point at compute (160,000 Huawei AI chips in a planned Inner Mongolia site), model development, and headcount; the listing is targeted for early 2027 — Bloomberg (tier-1).

DeepSeek stops being "the price-war lab" and starts being a public-market-funded compute incumbent — if your TCO model assumes V4-Pro stays the cheapest frontier-adjacent API through 2027, redo it with the pre-IPO repricing from August as the floor, not the ceiling.
