Lead Story

All Three AI Frontier Chiefs Head to the G7

For the first time, the leaders of OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and Anthropic will sit at the same international table. Sam Altman, Demis Hassabis, and Dario Amodei have all confirmed they will attend the G7 summit in Évian-les-Bains, France, running June 15–17, where artificial intelligence is set to dominate the agenda (Bloomberg).

The optics are hard to miss. A year ago these three were mostly trading barbs over benchmarks and poaching researchers; now they are being treated as quasi-diplomatic actors, invited alongside heads of state to help shape how the wealthiest democracies coordinate on frontier models. The summit comes at a moment when governments have stopped treating AI as a someday problem and started writing rules with teeth (Dataconomy).

Expect the conversation to center on three threads: a shared evaluation framework for dangerous-capability testing, compute and export coordination, and the perennial tension between open access and security. The companies arrive with leverage—they hold the models—but also exposure, since any voluntary commitments made on a stage like this become benchmarks they will be held to.

For builders, the meeting is less about the photo op and more about what gets standardized. If the G7 converges on a common pre-release review window or a shared incident-reporting format, that quietly becomes the global baseline every downstream API and deployment has to design around.

The analysis: When the people who ship the models start co-writing the rules that govern them, regulatory capture and genuine safety coordination look almost identical from the outside. The thing to watch is not what they promise in Évian, but whether the commitments come with verification anyone but the labs can run.

The hands-off era of AI oversight is ending in real time. A federal executive order, Colorado's AI Act taking effect June 30, and California's frontier-AI transparency law are converging on the same demand: show your safety work before you ship. For solo builders, the lesson isn't to panic—it's that "move fast" is quietly being replaced by "document as you move."
