Lead Story

SpaceX buys Cursor for $60 billion, turning a rocket company into an AI coding superpower

Just days after its record-setting IPO, SpaceX exercised an option to acquire Anysphere — the maker of the AI coding assistant Cursor — in a $60 billion all-stock deal expected to close in Q3 2026. Overnight, the company best known for landing boosters became one of the largest owners of developer-facing AI on the planet.

The logic runs through xAI, which SpaceX absorbed in February. AI coding is the rare frontier-AI category that already prints real enterprise revenue: Cursor crossed $1 billion in annualized revenue last November, riding the "vibe coding" wave it helped create. Folding Cursor's product and user base into xAI's models gives Musk's empire an instant foothold against OpenAI's Codex line and Anthropic's Claude Code — the two tools that have, until now, defined the category.

Founded in 2022 by MIT classmate Michael Truell and three co-founders, Cursor will become a wholly owned SpaceX subsidiary. Truell framed the move around "building the world's most useful AI models," and the strategic subtext is hard to miss: the company wants the full stack, from satellite-based compute down to the editor where code actually gets written. SpaceX shares kept climbing post-IPO, briefly pushing its market cap above Amazon's to make it the world's fifth-most valuable public company.

The read for builders: the AI coding tool you depend on is now a strategic asset inside a much larger war. Consolidation tends to be good for capability and bad for optionality. If your workflow is welded to one assistant, this is the week to confirm you could switch editors without losing your whole setup — and to watch whether pricing and model access shift once the deal closes.
