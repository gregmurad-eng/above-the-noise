Lead Story

Nvidia's Q2 print reprices the compute layer — and the memory bottleneck sets the ceiling — Nvidia

Nvidia reported fiscal Q2 FY27 after the close on Aug 26 and, in one release, reset the arithmetic that every hyperscaler and lab downstream is now running for Q4 planning. Revenue landed at $96.2B, up 106% year-over-year and 18% sequentially; Data Center hit $89.0B, up 117% YoY; GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins both at 75.0%; non-GAAP diluted EPS of $2.22.[1] Q3 guidance came in at $108B ±2% at 74.0% gross margin — $17B above Nvidia's own Q2 guide of $91B and roughly $4B above the sell-side consensus of $104B that Kiplinger's live tracker was reporting into the print.[1][2]

The two numbers that will move Q4 planning inside every hyperscaler are further down the release. Management guided FY28 to 70% year-over-year revenue growth, with Jensen Huang telling analysts on the call that the FY28 outlook "would be a lot higher" if not for supply constraints.[3] And supply commitments — Nvidia's own purchase obligations, mostly for future memory and components — more than doubled from $119B last quarter to $279B this quarter, disclosed as "primarily related to the procurement of memory."[4][5] CFO Colette Kress framed the demand environment against a $2T cloud-industry backlog, and said hyperscaler capex is tracking above $800B this year, scaling toward $1.3T in 2027.[3]

Huang framed the print with the sentence Wall Street will quote for weeks: "AI has reached its inflection point. It's doing useful work. Its tokens are productive and profitable. Now, compute is revenue."[1] He added the demand story has broadened — "This time last year, one lab alone was driving the buildout; today, we have a golden age of new AI labs and startups, multiple frontier labs scaling in parallel, a thriving open-model ecosystem and physical AI coming online" — and reaffirmed the "$3 trillion to $4 trillion AI infrastructure opportunity" framing over the next five years.[1][6]

The uncomfortable part sits under the guide. The $279B supply commitment is not headroom; it is Nvidia telling the market that the memory supply chain is now the binding constraint on FY28. TrendForce's early-August reporting on Nvidia considering a spec cut to Rubin Ultra's HBM configuration was the earliest tell; Kress's language on the call, and Seoul Economic Daily's coverage of the same-day commentary from HBM suppliers, both put the bottleneck lasting into 2028.[7] A hyperscaler that reads the print as "Nvidia guided up by $17B, so I have time" is reading it wrong — the correct read is that memory allocation is scarce, Nvidia is buying it forward at scale, and the compute customers who lock capex earliest get supply first.

If your Q4 inference-cost model was drafted before yesterday's close, freeze the assumption column and rebuild it against a $108B Q3 top-line, 74% gross margin, and a $279B supply commitment that is mostly memory — because at least one of your model providers is about to price memory scarcity into their next rate card.

OpenAI's o3 leaves ChatGPT today — OpenAI

OpenAI o3 was retired from ChatGPT on Aug 26 after a 90-day sunset that began May 28.[8] API access is unchanged — this is a ChatGPT-only removal, and existing conversations auto-continue on the corresponding GPT-5-family model. It is a small event on paper and the second frontier deprecation in ninety days, which is the actual story: the deprecation cadence at OpenAI is now fast enough that any workflow bound to a specific reasoning model needs a written fallback path.

If any of your prod agents, scheduled jobs, or Custom GPTs still name o3 in a system prompt or user preset, audit them this week and pin them to a GPT-5-family model with an explicit fallback — the ChatGPT-only sunset is a preview of the API deprecation window that will follow.

Ask Gemini in Google Chat begins its wider rollout — Google Workspace

Google's Workspace team confirmed Ask Gemini in Chat began rolling out on Aug 26 as the successor to the old Gemini side panel, with a Ctrl+G / ⌘+G command line that searches across Gmail, Drive, and Calendar, drafts from inside the conversation, and organises work into per-topic sessions.[9] Promotional higher usage limits run through October 1. English-language accounts get the new surface first; other language accounts keep the old side panel until support ships. Old side-panel history does not migrate automatically — Takeout or lose it.

If any team of yours has been storing threads or grounding in the old Gemini side panel as if it were durable, export via Takeout this week — the migration cutover is a data-loss risk that Google has explicitly not undertaken.

Meta launches AI Core and AI Premium subscription tiers — Social Media Today

Meta introduced two paid AI subscription packages on Aug 26 — Meta AI Core and Meta AI Premium — both bundling higher image- and video-generation quotas and expanded model access.[10] This is the first time Meta has priced consumer AI directly rather than treating it as a distribution feature of the platform. The framing matters: Meta's Q2 filings recorded elevated AI capex on the same call the plaintiff's bar was opening in Oakland on the 29-state child-safety trial, and paid tiers are how a big platform funds the buildout without further concentrating cost inside advertising.

EU AI Act enforcement passes its first month with the fine architecture live — European Commission

The Commission's AI Office and member-state market-surveillance authorities activated full enforcement powers on Aug 2, with the tiered fine structure now live: up to 3% of global revenue for general-purpose AI model violations, up to 7% or €35M (whichever is higher) for prohibited practices, and up to 3% or €15M for high-risk system breaches.[11][12] Any chatbot or voice agent deployed in the EU must now identify itself as AI at the start of interaction; AI-generated content requires machine-readable provenance labels. The Digital Omnibus (Regulation 2026/1744, signed July 27) pushed the conformity assessment for high-risk systems out to Dec 2, 2027 — buying time on the hard classification, not on the transparency clock.

If any of your production surfaces face EU users, you now have three unambiguous shipping requirements before Q4: an at-start AI disclosure, a machine-readable provenance marker on generated media, and a documented risk-classification decision that survives a Commission audit request.
