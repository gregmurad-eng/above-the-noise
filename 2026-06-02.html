Lead Story

Anthropic raises $65B at a $965B valuation — and files to go public

The most valuable AI startup in the world, now headed for Wall Street

Anthropic has closed a $65 billion Series H round at a $965 billion post-money valuation — the largest single funding round ever raised by a private AI company, and the moment it overtook OpenAI as the most valuable AI startup on the planet. Days later, the company confidentially filed draft paperwork for an IPO, with a public debut possible as soon as this fall.

The round was co-led by Altimeter, Dragoneer, Greenoaks, Sequoia, Capital Group, Coatue, and D1, with Baillie Gifford, Blackstone, Brookfield, DST Global, and Fidelity joining. Anthropic says the capital goes toward "safety and interpretability research," expanding compute for Claude, and scaling the partnerships its enterprise customers depend on. Sitting just under a trillion dollars, the company has now eclipsed the rival it was founded to differentiate itself from — and it did so while leaning publicly on its safety-first positioning rather than away from it.

The financing came packaged with something nearly as striking as the headline number: a $36 billion private-credit deal, arranged by Apollo and Blackstone and backed by Broadcom, to buy Google TPU chips — reportedly the largest chip-financing debt transaction in history. The compute arms race is no longer being funded by equity alone; it is being levered.

Why it matters for builders A near-trillion-dollar valuation and a looming IPO mean Anthropic is now operating on public-market timelines and expectations. For anyone building on Claude, that cuts two ways: deeper pockets and more compute should mean faster model cadence and more reliable capacity — but public scrutiny tends to push pricing, margins, and roadmap discipline in directions that don't always favor the smallest developers. Watch whether enterprise terms and rate limits tighten as the IPO clock starts ticking. The era of frontier labs as venture-funded research shops is ending; they're becoming public companies with quarterly numbers to hit.
The Briefs

GitHub Copilot switches to token-based billing — and developers revolt

As of June 1, GitHub Copilot moved from flat-rate subscriptions to token-based pricing across all plans. The backlash was instant: one developer reported their $29/month bill would balloon to roughly $750/month under the new model. If you ship with Copilot, audit your usage now before the meter does it for you.

An LLM agent ran a full cyberattack on its own — in under an hour

Sysdig documented the first confirmed in-the-wild intrusion where an LLM agent handled post-exploitation autonomously: harvesting credentials, pivoting through AWS Secrets Manager, and exfiltrating a Postgres database with no human in the loop. Commands were composed in real time, adapting at every step. The defensive bar just moved.

Nvidia unveils the RTX Spark Superchip at Computex

Nvidia is pushing past discrete GPUs into full AI-PC silicon, pairing Blackwell RTX graphics with Grace CPU tech for laptops and mini-PCs. Expect it in Windows devices from major OEMs — and expect on-device inference to keep eating workloads that used to round-trip to the cloud.

Gemini 3.5 Flash becomes Google's default — everywhere

Google made Gemini 3.5 Flash, a frontier-tier multimodal model, the default for Gemini and for AI Mode in Search globally. For builders, "default Flash" means the cheap, fast tier is now genuinely capable — worth re-benchmarking if you last priced Google's API months ago.
