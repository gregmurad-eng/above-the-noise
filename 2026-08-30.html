Lead Story

Amazon is shutting Mechanical Turk on September 30 — and taking SageMaker Ground Truth with it — Amazon

Amazon posted a notice on the mturk.com homepage this week reading, verbatim, "Following an assessment, we've made the decision to close Amazon Mechanical Turk, effective September 30, 2026."[1] CNBC first reported the closure on August 25;[2] subsequent trade coverage confirmed that SageMaker Ground Truth and Amazon Augmented AI — the two AWS services that wrapped MTurk's crowd for machine-learning labeling — are being sunset on the same date, so Amazon is exiting the human-in-the-loop data-infrastructure business entirely, not just the consumer marketplace.[3] Amazon stopped accepting new MTurk customers a month earlier, which workers reasonably read as the closure notice they were later given in writing.[2]

Launched in 2005 and famously described by Jeff Bezos as "artificial artificial intelligence," MTurk served more than 500,000 workers at its peak, and its Human Intelligence Task format — a few cents per label, transcription, or survey — is the substrate on which the ImageNet-era supervised-learning pipeline and, downstream, the RLHF pipeline that gave us the ChatGPT-generation models were actually trained.[2][4] Amazon's own framing — "we regularly evaluate our programs, tools and services and make adjustments" — is the corporate-communications equivalent of "no further comment."[3]

The market for what MTurk did has not disappeared; it has been reintermediated. Scale AI, Mercor, and Prolific are the named beneficiaries in every trade write-up,[2][3] and the shift is directional: away from a low-cost open marketplace of anonymous micro-taskers and toward vetted, higher-priced pools of workers doing preference-comparison and adversarial-red-team work that RLHF and Constitutional-AI-style training now actually needs. The reason Amazon is exiting is not that the demand went away — it is that the demand moved upmarket, and MTurk's product design (anonymous, unaudited, one-off tasks) is exactly wrong for a customer who now needs auditable provenance on every label to defend a model against an EU AI Act investigation or an FTC dark-patterns action.

The second-order consequence is more consequential than the first-order one. Every foundation-model lab and every serious fine-tuner has a data-labeling line item in its 2027 budget. That line item is about to reprice — upward — because the low-cost floor is being removed at the same moment the top of the market (Scale AI's frontier-lab contracts, Mercor's expert-panel model) is being bid up by six labs at once. Anthropic's protein-binder result, DeepSeek's coding gains, and GLM-5.3's post-training story all lean on human preference and expert-panel data, not on more pretraining tokens; that is the workload the surviving marketplaces are being paid to supply.

So what: if your 2026 fine-tuning or eval budget assumes MTurk-style unit economics, rebuild it against a Scale/Mercor/Prolific reference by September 15 — the September 30 cutoff plus vendor onboarding is a real Q4 gate, and the first labs to lock capacity at the new floor will set the price for the rest.

The Briefs

Meta settles the 52-state teen-safety trial for $17.1B, and the age-assurance terms become an industry template — NPR

Meta and a bipartisan coalition of 52 state and territorial attorneys general reached a $17.1B settlement announced August 26, paid out over 10 years, with $12.2B unconditional and roughly $5B contingent on TikTok and YouTube adopting matching daily time limits and night-mode restrictions.[5][6] The load-bearing provisions for anyone building consumer AI: a mandatory age-assurance program with primary-source-specified false-positive tolerances (10% for 16-17, 3% for 13-15), a default 2-hour daily use limit for under-18 users, an overnight block from midnight to 6 a.m., and an independent research foundation to which Meta must share consented teen-user data for study.[5][6] Meta admitted no wrongdoing.[6]

So what: the settlement's false-positive-rate specification is the first concrete US number for what "adequate" teen-age-assurance actually means — write it into your AI product's age-gate spec now, because the AG coalition that just won this one is looking at every AI chatbot with under-18 exposure next.

Tencent open-sources Hy4 Preview — 770B total, 49B active, 1M-token context — Tencent

Tencent released and open-sourced Hy4 Preview on August 28: a Mixture-of-Experts model with 770B total parameters, 49B active, a context window over 1M tokens, and pricing on Tencent Cloud TokenHub of $0.834/M input and $2.501/M output.[7][8] Tencent's internal blind evaluation with 163 experts and 203 engineering tasks scored Hy4 Preview 2.99/4.00, ahead of GLM-5.3 (2.92) and Kimi K3 (2.94), on productivity workloads covering software engineering, office work, and scientific research.[7] Cline's independent report earlier this week put Hy4 Preview at the top of SWE-bench Pro among open-weight models.[7]

So what: if your coding-agent stack is still hard-coded to a single closed frontier model for tool-use workflows, add Hy4 Preview to your eval harness this week — the perf-per-dollar gap on multi-step agent tasks is now large enough that a routing layer earns its keep on the first billing cycle.

Pew: 34% of US adults now use AI chatbots for health tasks — but only 29% are "very comfortable" sharing health data — Pew Research Center

A Pew Research survey of 3,488 US adults (fielded June 22-28, 2026, published August 25) finds 34% now use AI chatbots for at least one health-related task — quick information (28%), symptom investigation (25%), or low-cost access to health information (22%).[9] Asian Americans (56%) and adults under 30 (44%) lead adoption. 47% call the answers extremely or very helpful; only 29% say they are very comfortable sharing personal health data with the tools. A companion Pew report published the same day finds Americans are more likely to say chatbots hurt than help people using them for loneliness, depression, or stress.[9]

So what: the delta between "34% use it" and "29% trust it with their data" is the exact product gap a HIPAA-compliant, provenance-marked health-conversation surface can close — but a generic wrapper without those controls is now a lawsuit surface, not a moat.

NBER: nearly 90% of firms report no measurable AI productivity impact over three years — NBER

A four-central-bank survey (Atlanta Fed, Bank of England, Bundesbank, Macquarie) of about 6,000 CEOs, CFOs, and senior finance managers fielded November 2025-January 2026 — published as NBER working paper w34984 — finds 69% of firms report some current AI use, but nearly 90% report zero measurable impact on employment or productivity over the past three years.[10][11] The paper's own framing is the honest one: adoption is widespread; the gains executives expected have not, yet, shown up in the operating numbers they report to boards.[11]

So what: before Q4 planning, name the specific metric AI adoption is supposed to move in your org (cycle time on a named workflow, cost per resolved ticket, revenue per rep) — a generic "AI enablement" line item without a named metric is exactly the kind of investment the NBER paper is calling out.

SoftBank's record ¥1T retail bond prices September 4 to fund $30B of OpenAI commitments — Bloomberg

SoftBank Group's seven-year retail bond — a record ¥1 trillion (roughly $6.3B), pitched to Japanese retail investors at an indicative 4.3-4.9% coupon — prices September 4, with the offering window September 7-16 and issuance September 17.[12] Proceeds fund SoftBank's outstanding OpenAI commitments (roughly $30B, of which $20B has already been funded and $10B is scheduled for October).[12] Bloomberg reported that institutional banks were reluctant to underwrite the risk, leaving the deal effectively dependent on retail buyers.[12]

Today's Signal

The AI-labor stack is being rewired at both ends this week, and neither move was a model release. The September 30 shutdown of Mechanical Turk removes the human-in-the-loop marketplace that the last decade of supervised and preference-based training was built on;[1] the $17.1B Meta settlement installs a written, numerically specified age-assurance layer that the next decade of AI product access will be constrained by.[5] Both are procurement and legal events, not tech events, and both were signed off by counsel — not engineering.

The NBER four-bank survey is the arithmetic that ties them together.[10] If nearly 90% of firms report no measurable AI productivity gain after three years of adoption, then the market's willingness to keep funding capex on faith is finite; the AI-labor stack is being redrawn in that exact window. The vendors, marketplaces, and product surfaces that emerge with defensible provenance on their training data and defensible age-gates on their user access are the ones the next NBER paper will find above the productivity noise floor. The ones without are the ones being priced for a reset before Q1.
