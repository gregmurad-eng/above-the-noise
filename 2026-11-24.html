Lead Story

NVIDIA's board unanimously reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO with US government "input" on the vote — NVIDIA[1][2]

Twelve days after accepting his resignation, NVIDIA's board on 23 November unanimously voted to reinstate Jen-Hsun Huang as president, CEO and director, effective immediately[1]. The Form 8-K filed with the SEC on 22 November describes the reversal in narrow legal language, but NVIDIA's own release goes further: it says the board "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and concluded on that basis that Huang's return "poses no national security risk"[1][2]. President Trump, in remarks carried by PBS and CNBC, said he was "represented in the board meeting" and framed his intervention as decisive[4][3]. NVIDIA shares opened 31%+ higher on the news[3].

The resignation on 12 November — accepted the same day by the board, per the earlier 8-K — came after reporting of two affairs, one of them with a person the board later described as "the target of a foreign intelligence effort"[1][5]. An independent committee was stood up; a new Chief Compliance Officer search is still under way[1]. The board's current position is that the investigation ran to conclusion, that it is "satisfied" with Huang's candor, and that the national-security question has been resolved — the latter explicitly with executive-branch input on the record[1].

Analytically, this is less a corporate-governance story than a compute-governance one. NVIDIA is the single supplier whose GPUs underwrite roughly every frontier training run under way in the West, and whose roadmap determines the cost curve every lab prices its next capital raise against. When the question "who runs this company" is settled in a boardroom with the executive branch "represented," the compute pipeline's dependency tree now officially includes the White House — an input condition that was implicit through export controls and foreign-investment review, and is now explicit in a primary disclosure. The 8-K will be cited as the first filing of its kind for years.

For builders, the first-order effect is boring: roadmaps, pricing, and GTC cadence under Huang are unchanged. The second-order effect is the one to price: the terms of trade between frontier labs and their one indispensable supplier are now a governed relationship, not a commercial one, and the governing is being done on a timeline measured in days, not quarters. The 33% move on the open was the market noticing.

Treat NVIDIA roadmap risk as political-timeline risk now, not just chip-supply risk — assume any future executive-order on compute could pass through the same backchannel in the same timeframe, and keep an AMD/Trainium fallback at least theoretically costed for anything mission-critical shipping past Q1 2027.

The Briefs

OpenAI's chief scientist proposes shared perplexity-based limits on frontier development — OpenAI[6][7]

Jakub Pachocki's "Perplexity and Intelligence" post on 22 November published OpenAI's own pretraining log-loss curve for the first time (0.99 → 0.76) and proposed "common limits, based on perplexity and other measurements, that developers are committed to not crossing" until monitoring-and-alignment capability reaches a concrete milestone[6]. Bloomberg and TechCrunch covered it as the first time a frontier lab has nominated a specific, publicly-measurable scalar as a shared pacing variable[7][8]. Sam Altman called it "the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while"[7].

If a 0.68–0.70 ceiling is adopted by peers on a sliding window, any roadmap that assumes next-gen capability jumps keep arriving on the current cadence is now exposed to a voluntary pause — stress-test your 2027 planning against a 6–12 month flatline.

Google DeepMind's Gemini 4 agents cut quantum arithmetic circuits up to 290×, every result Lean-verified — Google DeepMind + Google Quantum AI[9][10]

Teams of Gemini 4 agents synthesised quantum programs against formal specifications and had each one proof-checked in Lean 4 before the result was accepted — 2.55× faster parallel search, 290× cheaper arithmetic, and 11×/8×/2.5× gains on fusion, photochemistry and drug-metabolism simulations[10][9]. DeepMind open-sourced the Lean 4 framework and problem library; the agent orchestration stayed internal[10].

The template — frontier model proposes, theorem prover disposes — is the first credible answer to "how do you let an agent do novel engineering work without having to trust it"; adopt it anywhere your agent's output has to be audit-grade.

Amazon Threat Intelligence: China-linked Cairn agents found a MONAI zero-day and had root in 11 minutes — Amazon / Dark Reading[11][12]

Amazon's 20 November report documents autonomous agents running the open-source Cairn framework discovering CVE-2026-133968 in MONAI Label on an East Asian university server and reaching arbitrary file write in roughly 11 minutes, then root[12][11]. CISA has added the CVE to its KEV catalog; Amazon attributed the campaign with moderate confidence to a China-linked actor on the basis of Simplified Chinese prompts and CST timestamps[12].

Zero-day-to-root in 11 minutes is faster than most SOC's mean-time-to-detect; if you ship any public service, shrink the window between dependency CVE publication and your patch rollout to hours, not days.

AWS ships Strands Decider v2 — five Apache-2.0 "picker" models built on Gemma 4 — AWS / SiliconANGLE[13][14]

AWS released five open-weight decision models (2B / 4B / 12B / 26B-A4B MoE / 31B dense) that do not generate text — given a state and a question, they return a probability distribution over a fixed option set. Reported six-benchmark text average rose from 70.0 (v1 26B-A4B) to 82.1 (v2 31B)[13][14]. Weights are on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0.

For any agent loop where the hard question is "which tool, which branch, which escalation?", a small cheap classifier is often a better fit than a large generative call — the Decider line makes that trade cheap enough to default to.

First Loop Transformer flagship at ~750B scale: the 28 layers of the GLM-5 base run twice per forward pass with shared weights, plus gated activation updates, hybrid KV cache, and reused sparse-attention indices. Z.ai claims intelligence comparable to 2T–3T parameter dense models, 200 tok/s off-peak, 1M context. Live in GLM Coding Plan / ZCode / Z.ai Chat. Thinking Machines Inkling 2 Mini — 26.4B open-weight looped MoE for laptops — Thinking Machines Lab (20 Nov 2026)[18]

Apache 2.0 multimodal MoE, 26.4B total / 3.4B active per token, middle 12 of 24 layers loop 3× (≈5.4B effective active), 1M context, NVFP4 weights + MXFP8 last-four-layer, ~20GB memory footprint. Leads <100B open-weights on DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%) and Vals Index v2.2 (46.5%). Weights on Hugging Face.
Builder read: a credible on-laptop model in this size range changes the floor for how much you have to ship to a cloud at all — worth a prototype pass for any assistant feature currently paying per-token for a frontier model to do rote work.
