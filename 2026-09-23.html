Lead Story

Anthropic and OpenAI cut frontier prices on the same day

— Anthropic and OpenAI, 22 September 2026

On Monday, 22 September, Anthropic launched Claude Opus 5.5 at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output — twenty per cent below Opus 5 — with cache reads at $0.20 per million, sixty per cent cheaper[1]. Roughly an hour later, OpenAI shipped GPT‑6 Sol at $2 per million input / $10 output, and a small‑frontier sibling, GPT‑6 Luna, at $0.10 input / $0.50 output — half the introductory prices of the GPT‑5.6 family[2]. Two labs cut their headline sticker on the same trading day. That has not happened before.

The performance numbers are the second story, not the first. Anthropic says Opus 5.5 clears 66.4 % on Terminal‑Bench 4.0 and scored 1,846 Elo on GDPval‑AA v2.1, with one external tester completing a 680,000‑line code migration inside a day[1]. OpenAI positions Sol as capable of beating "Claude Opus 5 at max effort" on its own AutomationBench suite at nine per cent of the cost per task[2]. Read across two labs those framings are marketing, not evidence — but the cost sides are objective, and they move the floor.

Simon Willison, who tracks pricing more carefully than almost anyone outside the labs, called the new levels "hard to overstate how competitive" and flagged that GPT‑6 Luna is now beaten on price only by the far weaker GPT‑4.1 Nano and GPT‑5 Nano[4]. The wider trade read is that this is now a two‑horse price war with a distinct second race — the small‑frontier tier at $0.10 – $2 input — opening beneath it[3].

For builders, this is not "cheaper Claude, cheaper GPT." It is a compaction of the pricing curve. A batch job that ran on GPT‑5.6 Sol at introductory pricing is now half price on GPT‑6 Sol. A retrieval or classification path that could not justify a frontier model at Opus 5 rates can defensibly justify Opus 5.5. And Luna at $0.10 input drags the whole "cheap tier" conversation into new territory — a nightly agent loop with a million input tokens per run costs ten cents to run at rest.

One live footnote from Willison: Anthropic's "40 % cheaper" claim compares Opus 5 at its old default reasoning effort with Opus 5.5 at a new lower default. That is fair for most users, but existing code that pins reasoning_effort=high may 400 before it sees any saving[4]. Read the migration note before you flip the model string in production.

So what: today is the day to re‑price your production model math against Sol at $2 input and Opus 5.5 at $4 input — and to sanity‑check that your batch and eval jobs are not pinned to a reasoning‑effort flag that blocks the new default and eats the promised saving.

Today's Signal

Two frontier labs cut their headline prices on the same trading day, and the industry did not blink because the industry expected it. That is the actual news. The pricing curve is now compacting faster than either lab's marketing suggests: Opus 5.5 at $4 input, Sol at $2 input, Luna at $0.10 input, Grok 4.7 at $2 input. Six months ago three of those numbers didn't exist and the fourth was double.

The Council briefing today is the other side of the same coin. The labs are asking for slower capability growth in public, while shipping cheaper capability in private. Neither is dishonest — pacing is about safety headroom, and price is about compute economics — but the two levers are visible in the same 48 hours, and a builder should hold both in the same hand: model economics have changed enough this week to re‑cost every scheduled job; policy has not.
