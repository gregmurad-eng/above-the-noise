Lead Story

Anthropic cuts Haiku to a dime and meets OpenAI at the floor

Anthropic shipped Claude Haiku 5.5 on 7 October and set its price at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens for prompts under 100,000 tokens — a roughly 75% cut from Haiku 4.5 and an exact match to OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna, which began rolling out to Free and Go users of ChatGPT the following morning[1][2]. Above 100,000 tokens the price steps up to $0.50 / $2.50. Context remains 1M tokens, output up to 128K, and Anthropic is calling it "the cheapest, fastest, and most capable small model we've ever released"[3].

Two things make this more than a routine refresh. First, timing: Anthropic landed its price cut on the same day OpenAI began the Sol/Luna rollout of GPT-6 to paid tiers[4], and 24 hours before Luna reached free users. That is not a coincidence — the two labs now sit at an identical published floor for the small-and-fast slot, which is where high-volume routing lives (classification, embedding-adjacent lookups, agent sub-calls, bulk extraction). The commodity line has been drawn, and it is drawn at a dime.

Second, a hidden cost shift that is easy to miss. Simon Willison, reading the release the same day, flagged a tokenizer change: "the same long prompt uses around 1.25x as many tokens with Haiku 5.5 compared to Haiku 4.5"[5]. On a per-million-token basis Haiku is 75% cheaper. On a per-document basis the delta is smaller. Builders who are routing by cost need to re-benchmark their actual prompts, not accept the headline number.

What is new strategically: Anthropic is now explicitly selling against OpenAI at the price layer, not only the capability layer. For a year the pitch was "pay more for Opus/Sonnet quality." The pitch today is "same price as Luna under 100K, higher benchmark scores." That is a different kind of claim, and it is the first time in this cycle a frontier lab has openly priced to match a direct competitor's small-model floor rather than quietly undercut it. Pricing floors do not stay floors — they get tested.

If your production routing was built around Haiku 4.5 at $1/$5, you can cut spend ~75% on prompts under 100K by swapping the model slug — but re-run your token counts first; the new tokenizer eats ~25% more tokens per character on long inputs, which eats part of the win.

The Briefs

OpenAI brings GPT-6 to every ChatGPT tier — OpenAI

OpenAI began rolling GPT-6 across ChatGPT on 7 October: Sol for Plus/Pro/Business/Enterprise, Luna for Free and Go users starting 8 October[4]. Shipped alongside is "Intelligent UI," which lets the model respond with interactive charts, forms, tappable buttons and editable diagrams instead of text alone[6]. OpenAI frames it as "a significant shift in how people interact with ChatGPT."

Any product that embeds ChatGPT via the web share-sheet or iframe now has to assume responses may contain live widgets, not just prose — sanitise and style for it, or your "copy this answer" button will start copying JSON blobs.

Cantwell releases six-point frontier-AI framework — U.S. Senate Commerce

Senator Maria Cantwell (D-WA), ranking member of Senate Commerce, published a six-principle framework for frontier AI on 7 October: enforceable federal safety standards, continuous testing/audit, disclosure and oversight, public-private partnerships, protections for children and workers, and U.S.-led global cooperation[7]. NIST would set risk-based standards, including for cyber-attack, bio/nuclear uplift, loss of human control, and "rogue agents escaping secure testing environments"[8].

This is a draft bill in waiting — if you ship an agent that can execute code, assume a near-future compliance ask of "prove it can't escape its sandbox" and start keeping the evidence (seccomp profile, test logs, red-team runs) in a form a NIST auditor could read.

UN rights chief: "the clock on AI regulation is ticking" — UN News

UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Türk warned on 5 October that voluntary industry commitments are not enough and that mandatory safeguards are overdue, in a statement endorsing both the Global Dialogue on AI Governance and a joint call from 29 states plus the EU for tighter control of frontier models[9]. He singled out the risk of "entire populations… cut off from communication, clean water, heating and financial services at the click of a button."

If you sell AI into European or multilateral buyers, the ground is shifting from "we signed a voluntary pact" to "which independent audit cleared you" — update your enterprise security questionnaire boilerplate before an RFP forces you to.

Google Threat Intelligence Group's rolling study (Jan 2025–Aug 2026) found that half of likely-AI-discovered vulnerabilities enable remote code execution, versus 26% of those found by other means; monthly CVE disclosures roughly doubled across 2026, from 5,045 in January to 10,740 in August[10]. The window from "patch available" to "patch applied" is now measured in days, not weeks — if your dependency-update job runs weekly, that gap is your incident waiting to happen; move critical-severity patching to a daily cadence.

Voices

Simon Willison, writing on his weblog on 7 October 2026, hours after Anthropic shipped Haiku 5.5[5]:

"If your workloads fit in 100,000 tokens, Haiku is the same price as Luna and reports higher benchmark scores. Above 100,000 tokens, Luna looks like a much better deal."

Willison is the most consistently useful practitioner voice reading model pricing in public, and his read on Haiku 5.5 is unusually blunt: the two labs are now priced identically for the common case, and the choice collapses to context length rather than vendor loyalty. He also flagged the tokenizer change (same prompt, ~1.25× tokens) — the kind of fine print that moves real production budgets but rarely makes a launch post.

Add "run the real prompt distribution through both tokenizers" to any Haiku 5.5 vs. Luna bakeoff before you decide — Willison's 1.25× number means a cost comparison done on model prices alone will mislead you by roughly a quarter.

Today's Signal

Frontier labs just drew a commodity line under the small-fast model, and they drew it together. Anthropic matching OpenAI's $0.10/$0.50 price inside 48 hours is not a race to the bottom — it is two labs agreeing, in public, that the floor of this market is a dime. What differentiates small models from here on is not price but context length, tokenizer efficiency, and routing cost at scale. The pricing fight has moved up the stack: Opus-tier reasoning and agent orchestration is where the margin lives now, and the small-fast layer is becoming utility.
