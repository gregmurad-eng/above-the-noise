Lead Story

GitLab patches a CVSS 9.9 flaw in its AI Gateway — a prompt-template sandbox escape that lets a logged-in Duo Agent user run arbitrary commands on the host — GitLab security advisory, 2 October[1]

GitLab disclosed CVE-2026-90970 on October 2 and rated it 9.9 of 10 — the Critical severity bucket reserved for unauthenticated or near-unauthenticated remote code execution. The vulnerability lives in the custom-flow prompt template inside GitLab's AI Gateway: an authenticated user with Duo Agent Platform access could craft a flow configuration that "escapes the prompt-template sandbox," producing arbitrary command execution on the Gateway host.[1][2] Affected versions span 18.1.6 through 19.2.3, 19.3.0–19.3.1, and 19.4.0; the fix shipped as 19.2.4, 19.3.2 and 19.4.1. The flaw was reported by HackerOne researcher invisiblemeerkat.[1]

Who has to act and who does not matters here. GitLab-hosted AI Gateways — the ones serving GitLab.com, GitLab Dedicated and self-managed instances that use GitLab's hosted gateway — were patched before the advisory went public. The customers who must act today are self-hosted AI Gateway operators: anyone running the Duo Agent Platform in their own VPC against a GitLab-run or on-prem GitLab instance.[1][2] CISA added the record on the same day with an exploitation assessment of "none" at the time of disclosure — which is the normal starting point, not an all-clear.[3]

This is the lead rather than the Model Watch footer because the shape of the bug is the shape of a whole category. The Gateway is the standard LLM-proxy pattern: it accepts a user-authored prompt template, interpolates variables, forwards the result to a frontier model, and runs the model's structured tool-calls against local resources. "Escaping the prompt-template sandbox" means the user-authored side of that pipeline reached the host process. Every self-hostable gateway on the market — LiteLLM, Portkey, Helicone, LangGraph Platform, Flowise, OpenRouter's self-host, Vercel AI Gateway, Cloudflare AI Gateway — ships a similar template surface, and CVE-2026-42271 landed the exact same class of flaw in LiteLLM earlier this quarter.[4] Yesterday's edition led with Google Threat Intelligence Group's finding that 50% of 2026's AI-related CVEs now live in orchestration middleware, with 347% YoY growth.[5] CVE-2026-90970 is the version of that trend wearing a critical label and a vendor name.

Three things are worth noticing about the fix. First, GitLab shipped it through its normal patch cadence rather than treating the AI Gateway as a special-handling product, which is the right institutional signal — the gateway is production infrastructure, not an experiment. Second, the vulnerable component is the custom flow template, i.e. the feature designed to let customers extend the agent. The flexibility surface is the attack surface, and that trade-off is now priced in. Third, the fact that only authenticated users with Duo Agent Platform access can trigger the bug does less work than it looks like it does: in most enterprise deployments, every engineer with CI/CD rights has that permission.

If your platform runs any self-hosted LLM gateway, treat the prompt-template layer as an untrusted-input-to-host-process boundary this week — read your gateway's release notes for anything named template, flow or sandbox, inventory who can author or edit flow templates, and audit the service account the gateway runs under for anything that would let an escape pivot to data stores or build runners.

The Briefs

Governor Newsom signs 13 AI bills, including the first US ban on using AI as the sole basis to fire or discipline workers — California Governor's Office[6][7]

Signed September 30 and taking effect across 2026–27. Headline bill: SB 947 (McNerney) prohibits employers from only relying on AI for disciplinary or termination decisions; a human has to corroborate, and the employee has to be told in writing when AI was "primarily used." AB 1221 and AB 1331 require advance notice before workplace surveillance tools (including AI monitoring) are introduced and ban surveillance in restrooms. SB 503 and AB 1979 impose human-oversight requirements on healthcare AI. SB 574 prohibits attorneys from delegating "core legal work" to AI. The package is the broadest state-level worker-AI framework in the US to date.[6][7]

If your product sits inside an HR, legal or clinical workflow sold into California, the "AI decided" path now carries a written-notice and human-corroboration obligation — add a per-decision provenance field to your audit log before the first enforcement cycle, because reconstructing it from logs after the fact will be the expensive version.

Google updated its Gemini support document October 3 with a new tier matrix taking effect October 9. Free users: Flash-Lite only (losing 3.6 Flash and the limited 3.1 Pro access they had). Related: Nvidia — Open Agent Safety Platform (OpenShell + Sentry) — launched September 28[15]

Not a model, but it is the layer that will sit between models and infrastructure in Nvidia-shop deployments. Voices

Simon Willison, co-creator of Django and the most-cited independent practitioner on LLM operations, in his October 3 post "We're going to need default hard budget caps on pretty much everything"[16]:

"Hard budget caps need to be the default."

— Simon Willison, 3 October 2026

Willison's argument is short and shaped by what he keeps seeing in his own and his readers' bills: cloud consoles and LLM dashboards offer "budget alerts" that notify you after you've already blown through the number, and the pattern transfers directly to agent runtimes, where a single mis-scoped loop can burn thousands of dollars overnight. His position is that soft caps belong to a world where humans sat at terminals; a world where agents fire thousands of API calls per minute needs the inverse default — the system refuses further billable work once a stated ceiling is reached, and the operator opts in to removing the ceiling, not out of enforcing it. Lands the same week GitLab shipped a critical gateway RCE and OpenAI's cheapest cached-input price fell to $0.10/M, both of which make the per-run blast radius of a rogue agent larger, not smaller.

Before the next weekend you run an agent unattended, verify that every billable surface in your stack — frontier-model API, vector DB, serverless compute, outbound SMS/email, pay-per-use third parties — has an enforced ceiling your code cannot override; if even one does not, that is the one that will cost you four figures the next time something loops.
