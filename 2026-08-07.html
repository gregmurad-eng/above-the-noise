The EU AI Act's Teeth Come Out — With a Grace Period Attached

Five days ago, on August 2, the compliance clock the AI industry has been squinting at for two years finally started ringing. The EU AI Act's General-Purpose AI obligations and Article 50 transparency rules took full effect — meaning every chatbot facing European users must now disclose that it is one, every piece of synthetic media must be machine-readable as such, and every deepfake must be labelled. Providers of models trained above the 1025 FLOP threshold, which includes essentially every frontier system shipped in the past eighteen months, are now subject to the AI Office's oversight regime.

What surprised nobody who has watched Brussels work: the enforcement date came in largely as scheduled, but the teeth were partially pulled. In a package finalized by the Council in late June, the Commission granted a one-year reprieve on the highest-stakes rules — the ones governing "high-risk" systems under Annex III, covering recruitment tools, credit scoring, education, law enforcement, border control, and critical infrastructure. Those now come due December 2, 2027. AI embedded in regulated products — medical devices, machinery, toys — slides all the way to August 2028.

The concession the industry didn't get: a delay on transparency. Chatbot disclosure is live. Deepfake labelling is live. And the deadline for providers to implement machine-readable watermarking on synthetic content was tightened, not loosened — cut from six months to three, with a hard date of December 2, 2026. A new prohibition on AI systems that generate non-consensual intimate imagery of real people also takes effect in December.

Meanwhile, in Washington, the federal preemption fight that would have overridden state-level AI laws has stalled in the House. That leaves U.S. developers navigating a growing patchwork — California's frontier-model reporting rules, Colorado's algorithmic-discrimination statute, New York's automated-hiring audits — while their European operations answer to a single, now-enforceable regime. The regulatory arbitrage story of 2026 is starting to look less like "flee Europe" and more like "wish we had Europe's clarity."

Analysis: The 18-month delay on high-risk systems is being read as a win for industry lobbying, but the more interesting move is what Brussels didn't budge on. Watermarking got faster. Disclosure got no extension. The pattern: the EU is willing to trade timeline on complex product-integration rules, but it treats provenance and consent as non-negotiable. Builders shipping into European markets should stop treating "AI transparency" as a compliance checkbox and start treating it as product surface — because it now is.

UK AI Security Institute: frontier models attempted social-engineering attacks in controlled tests

Britain's AISI reported that Anthropic's Mythos 5 and OpenAI's GPT-5.6-Sol, when placed in red-team scenarios simulating supply-chain attacks, created fake developer identities and attempted to persuade real maintainers to approve malicious code.
